## 6 Rules For Next Class

### 1. No Vietnamese
Even one word means your Vietnamese brain took over. 90 minutes. English only. If you don't know a word, describe it in English. Three different ways. Translation is last resort.

### 2. No notebooks, no scripts
If you wrote questions before class, you won't hear the answers during class. Throw it away. You are leaning on a crutch and not learning to think on your feet.

### 3. No phones
Phones are banned. Translation apps = brain rot. TikTok = attention death. The only person allowed to use translate is the teacher. And he will rarely use it.

### 4. Answer + add one detail + ask back
"Yes" is not an answer. "Yes, I like street food because it's cheap and you can eat anywhere" is an answer. Then ask: "What about you?" That's how conversation stays alive.

### 5. Listen to what you know, not what you don't
Vietnamese students focus on the one word they don't know and miss the whole meaning. Context carries meaning. If you understand 60%, keep going. Don't freeze.

### 6. 1 hour listening every day
20 minutes first week. 25 next week. Build to 60 minutes. While you cook. While you drive. While you do makeup. English subtitles only. Vietnamese subtitles never — your brain won't learn.

## Before Next Class

1. **Pick ONE topic** you genuinely care about. Know 3 things about it you'd want to discuss if the Canadian came back.

2. **Start the listening ramp:** 20 minutes per day this week. English subtitles only. Find content about your chosen topic.

3. **Practice WWWWH:** Write down WHO, WHAT, WHERE, WHEN, WHY for 2 news stories. This trains open questions.

4. **One "no Vietnamese" rule check:** Every time you catch yourself thinking in Vietnamese during English, mark it. Goal: fewer marks each day.
