## Cultural / Usage Notes

- "How's it going?" = just a greeting, not a real question (in Canada)
- Complimenting Vietnamese people can feel awkward — they often deflect ("No, I'm normal")
- Vietnamese rarely say "sorry" unless truly at fault or afraid of consequences
- Canadians say "sorry" automatically, often insincerely — Mai Tram is more polite than average
- "Lose face" = being embarrassed in front of others = big deal in SEA culture

## 10 Tips for Mai Tram

1. **Use "bonus" confidently** — You already know the work context. Add "performance bonus" and "quarterly bonus" to your active vocab.

2. **Master "team leader / supervisor"** — Both are correct for Miss Quinn. Use whichever feels natural; you can also say "my manager" in general.

3. **Stop self-editing "me vs I"** — You already self-correct mid-sentence. Trust your first instinct; fluency beats perfect grammar in real conversation.

4. **Use natural connectors** — Teacher noticed you understand "because", "so", "and then". Practice chaining thoughts with these instead of short fragments.

5. **Accept compliments with "Thank you"** — You're learning to deflect less. When someone says "good job", just say "Thank you. I practiced a lot." That's the modest-but-confident balance.

6. **Don't over-apologize** — You apologize more than most Vietnamese (class noted). Save "sorry" for when you actually did something wrong. It's a strength, not a weakness.

7. **Remember the brake story** — Real vocabulary sticks when attached to emotion. "Main brake" and "hand brake" are now YOUR words because you lived them. Use that technique: link new words to real memories.

8. **Practice "mature" usage** — You used "mature" naturally mid-class. That's a high-level adjective. Keep using it to describe people, situations, decisions.

9. **Reduce "um / uh / I I I" stalls** — When you feel stuck, pause instead of stuttering. A 2-second silence looks confident; "I I I" sounds unsure.

10. **Keep the curiosity** — You asked good clarifying questions ("So why do you have to do it many months ahead?"). That's how real conversations work. Don't mute that instinct.
