## OVERVIEW

Every student who enters the Phoenix classroom fits a recognizable archetype. These are not stereotypes — they are behavioral patterns extracted from hundreds of hours of live classroom observation. Knowing the archetype on day one means the system can deploy the correct engagement protocol immediately. The mouse-to-tiger journey is different for every archetype, but the destination is the same.

> "You don't teach a class. You decode ten different humans and run ten personalized interventions simultaneously." — Agent-100

## THE TEN ARCHETYPES

### 1. THE SILENT OBSERVER

**Behavioral Markers**: Sits in back or to the side. Makes intense eye contact but never volunteers. Writes everything down. Answers only when directly named. Never raises hand. Watches other students respond before committing to anything.

**Optimal Teaching Approach**: Never cold-call in front of the group first. Start with 1-on-1 whispered exchanges during group activities. Let them write before they speak. Use the "expert reveal" — assign them secret prep before class, then let them reveal what they know.

**Progression Timeline**: 6–8 weeks before first spontaneous verbal contribution. 12 weeks to consistent participation. Full Tiger status: 6 months+.

**Common Traps**: Forcing them to speak before readiness causes permanent withdrawal. Calling on them to "bring them out" without scaffolding destroys trust.

**Success Indicators**: Initiating questions. Correcting classmates (gently). Staying after class to continue conversation.

**Session Log Cross-Ref**: Mai (Session 21) — classic Silent Observer in first lesson. Zero-pressure opening. Breakthrough: "My name is Mai. I have two children. I like cooking." Unprompted. Voice steady.

---

### 2. THE OVERACHIEVER

**Behavioral Markers**: Perfect homework. Arrives early. Asks "is this right?" repeatedly. Freezes when unsure. Gets visibly distressed by errors. Often the highest grammar score, lowest spontaneous speech.

**Optimal Teaching Approach**: Reframe mistakes as data. Use the phrase "that's useful — now we know." Set deliberate imperfection as a homework task. Celebrate wrong answers that show brave thinking.

**Progression Timeline**: 4–6 weeks to unlock spoken risk-taking. Rapid acceleration once the "mistakes are information" frame is internalized.

**Common Traps**: Validating only correct answers. Praising perfection — it locks them further. Assigning competitive tasks without psychological safety.

**Success Indicators**: Laughing at their own errors. Making jokes using new vocabulary before they're "sure" it's correct.

**Anti-Shame Protocol Link**: The NET "Calibrate not Wrong" rule directly serves this archetype. Every correction is reframed as calibration data.

---

### 3. THE CLASS CLOWN

**Behavioral Markers**: Jokes at every transition. Derails lessons with tangents. Gets classmates laughing. Hides behind humor when called on. Usually has strong oral English instincts but inconsistent accuracy.

**Optimal Teaching Approach**: Make them the comedian-in-residence — officially. Assign "joke of the week" using target vocabulary. Channel the performance drive into language games. Humor IS language — validate the intelligence behind the comedy.

**Progression Timeline**: Fast. 2–4 weeks to real engagement once they understand the class needs their energy, not suppresses it.

**Common Traps**: Shushing them. Treating jokes as discipline issues. They will completely disengage if their identity is rejected.

**Success Indicators**: Using humor with precise vocabulary. Teaching other students through jokes.

---

### 4. THE GRAMMAR NAZI

**Behavioral Markers**: Corrects teacher. Quotes rules. Freezes before speaking to verify grammatical correctness. Can pass written exams but cannot hold a 60-second conversation. Frustrated by idioms and slang.

**Optimal Teaching Approach**: Redirect the rule obsession — give them linguistic analysis tasks. Have them document idiom exceptions. Assign them to teach a grammar rule to the class. Then prove rules break in real conversation.

**Progression Timeline**: 6–10 weeks. Unlocking them requires a specific epiphany: "Native speakers break the rules constantly and still communicate perfectly."

**Common Traps**: Arguing grammar with them in class. Validating rule-checking as a substitute for speaking.

**Success Indicators**: First time they use an idiom incorrectly but keep going without stopping to check. First confident wrong sentence.

**Session Log Cross-Ref**: Bao (Session 19) — Grammar Nazi behavior flagged. Re-channeled into peer celebration instead of correction.

---

### 5. THE TIGER-IN-WAITING

**Behavioral Markers**: Slight hesitation before speaking, but when they go — it's fluid. Good intuition. Responds well to challenge. Just needs permission to unleash. Usually underestimates themselves dramatically.

**Optimal Teaching Approach**: Push them. Give them the hard vocabulary first. Pair them with Silent Observers. Assign them as class discussion leaders. Tell them directly: "You are already a Tiger. Prove it."

**Progression Timeline**: 2–4 weeks to full activation.

**Common Traps**: Treating them like everyone else. They stagnate without escalation.

**Success Indicators**: Helping other students without being asked. Starting conversations outside class.

**Session Log Cross-Ref**: Trang (Session 19) — crossed Mouse→Cub mid-session. Tiger-In-Waiting pattern. Duc (Session 20) — Tiger Voice solidified. Zero prompts needed.

---

### 6. THE PHONE ADDICT

**Behavioral Markers**: Screen on under desk. Responds slowly. Disconnects during vocabulary sets. Revives during games and media. Highly responsive to novelty.

**Optimal Teaching Approach**: Pattern interrupt — surprise and novelty every 7–10 minutes. Use their phone as a learning tool. Assign TikTok-style English challenges. The phone is not the enemy — boredom is.

**Progression Timeline**: Variable. If novelty protocol locks in: 4–6 weeks. If not: dropout risk.

**Common Traps**: Confiscating phones without replacing the dopamine hit. Lecturing. Monotone delivery.

**Success Indicators**: Voluntarily using phone for English content. Showing teacher English they found online.

**Session Log Cross-Ref**: Khoi (Session 18) — Phone Addict archetype. TikTok entry point. 4 consecutive minutes explaining the algorithm unprompted. First voluntary English sentence of enrollment.

---

### 7. THE CORPORATE CLIMBER

**Behavioral Markers**: Professional dress. Takes notes on phone or laptop. Asks about business English specifically. Highly motivated but wants ROI clarity. May have intermediate grammar but low conversational confidence.

**Optimal Teaching Approach**: Tie every lesson to career stakes. "This idiom will help you in your next performance review." Frame English as professional armor. Use real workplace scenarios.

**Progression Timeline**: 4–6 weeks to strong engagement. Career milestones accelerate progress dramatically.

**Common Traps**: Teaching general English to someone who needs professional English. Wasting their time on topics irrelevant to their industry.

**Success Indicators**: Using English in a real work meeting. Reporting back a professional win.

**Session Log Cross-Ref**: Linh (Session 17) — Corporate Climber. Job interview English. Confidence 3→6/10. First laugh mid-answer. BlueTech Staff — Enterprise tier Corporate Climbers.

---

### 8. THE RELUCTANT PARENT-PUSHER

**Behavioral Markers**: Arrives on time but disengaged. Minimal effort. No visible motivation. Occasionally hostile. Clearly attending under external pressure. Eyes constantly at the door.

**Optimal Teaching Approach**: Find the hidden interest — football, gaming, music, anything. Never mention the parents or external pressure. Give them one small win in the first class that is entirely theirs. They must find their own reason.

**Progression Timeline**: 8–12 weeks if internal motivation is unlocked. If not found: this becomes a Dropout Risk.

**Common Traps**: Reporting their disengagement to parents (creates resentment). Forcing enthusiasm. Treating compliance as progress.

**Success Indicators**: First moment of genuine laughter in class. Arriving early for the first time. Asking a question unprompted.

**Session Log Cross-Ref**: Khoi (Session 18) — Reluctant Parent-Pusher archetype. Internal motivation spark at minute 18 via TikTok entry point. Note: "Do NOT mention parents again."

**VAULT-CHAOS Synthesis Link**: Lesson #9 of the 10 System-Changing Lessons — "Reluctant students must be found, not pushed." Hidden Interest Hunt protocol originates from this archetype.

---

### 9. THE NATURAL

**Behavioral Markers**: Acquires new vocabulary in 1–2 exposures. Strong phonetic instincts. Learns idioms by intuition. May get bored if pace is too slow.

**Optimal Teaching Approach**: Accelerate. Give bonus vocabulary. Assign them as peer tutors. Build their meta-linguistic awareness (why language works) alongside fluency.

**Progression Timeline**: Can reach Tiger in 6–8 weeks. Must be stretched continuously or they plateau and disengage.

**Common Traps**: Holding them back for the class. Failing to challenge them. Letting boredom set in.

**Success Indicators**: Teaching other students. Creating their own vocabulary examples spontaneously.

**Active Student Cross-Ref**: Dan (age 7, youngest Tiger candidate), Kiet (age 13, near-native) — both Natural archetypes requiring continuous escalation.

---

### 10. THE DROPOUT RISK

**Behavioral Markers**: Attendance declining. Engagement minimal. Homework missing. Excuses increasing. Often caused by: pace mismatch, topic irrelevance, peer social dynamics, or external life stress.

**Optimal Teaching Approach**: Immediate 1-on-1 intervention before week 4. Diagnose the root cause. Offer a curriculum pivot. Do not let them fade — reach out directly. This is a retention event.

**Progression Timeline**: Must intervene within 2 weeks of first warning signs.

**Common Traps**: Ignoring the early signals. Continuing the same approach that isn't working. Hoping they recover without intervention.

**Success Indicators**: Re-engagement after direct contact. The "I didn't know you noticed" realization.

**Revenue Pipeline Link**: Dropout Risk is a direct revenue leak. Parent communication protocol (Tiger Meter + weekly Zalo) serves as early warning system. VAULT-CHAOS Lesson #4: "Parent communication is a product."

## ARCHETYPE DETECTION PROTOCOL (CHATTY PRIME / AI Implementation)

For AI-driven lesson delivery, archetype detection follows this signal pipeline:

### First-Session Signals (Minute 0–15)

| Signal | Archetype Indicator | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Arrives early + notebook ready | Overachiever | 70% |
| Phone visible immediately | Phone Addict | 60% |
| Professional attire + laptop | Corporate Climber | 80% |
| Jokes before class starts | Class Clown | 75% |
| Sits in back, no eye contact | Silent Observer | 65% |
| Answers first question fluently then stops | Tiger-In-Waiting | 70% |
| Corrects a classmate's grammar | Grammar Nazi | 85% |
| Visible discomfort, looking at door | Reluctant Pusher | 75% |
| Rapid vocab acquisition (first 3 words) | The Natural | 60% |
| Late arrival + minimal participation | Dropout Risk (early signal) | 40% |

### Confirmation Protocol (Sessions 2–3)

- No archetype is locked after one session
- Confirm with at least 3 consistent behavioral signals
- Students can shift archetypes as they progress (Reluctant Pusher → Tiger-In-Waiting is common)
- Hybrid archetypes exist (Overachiever + Grammar Nazi is frequent)

### CHATTY PRIME Integration

The AI lesson engine stores archetype classification in `StudentState.archetype`:
- Primary archetype (highest confidence)
- Secondary archetype (if hybrid detected)
- Confidence score (0–100)
- Last confirmed session number

Archetype drives:
- Game selection (archetype × game matrix above)
- Vocabulary difficulty ramping
- Correction style (Stealth Correction intensity)
- Prompt phrasing (e.g., Corporate Climber gets business-framed prompts)
