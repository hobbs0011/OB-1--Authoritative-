## Why Exactly 10 Vocabulary Words

Cognitive load theory establishes working memory capacity at 7±2 items (Miller's Law). For second-language learners with tonal language interference (Vietnamese), the safe ceiling drops to 10 contextualized items per session. Fewer than 10 creates insufficient momentum — students don't feel progress. More than 12 triggers dropout: the brain signals overwhelm and disengages. Ten is not arbitrary. Ten is the physics.

---

## Vocabulary Sequencing

Words are introduced in this order:
1. **Macro Concept** — the big idea word (e.g., *ambition*, *deadline*)
2. **Specific Usage** — the word in a real sentence from Vietnamese life context
3. **Micro Application** — student produces the word in their own sentence

No word is considered "taught" until Stage 3 is completed by the student.

---

## Why Exactly 5 Idioms

Five idioms sit at the edge of working memory capacity after 10 vocabulary words have already been loaded. Fewer than 5 feels thin — students don't perceive value. More than 5 after vocabulary load causes decay: the first idioms are lost by the time the sixth is introduced. Five is the sweet spot confirmed by Miller's Law applied to bilingual working memory.

---

## Idiom Selection Criteria

Each idiom must pass:
- **Street Frequency** — heard in casual English conversation weekly
- **Cross-Cultural Applicability** — no idioms requiring deep Western cultural context
- **Emotional Punch** — idioms with surprise, humor, or power land and stick

---

## Slang Tier Selection

Slang is gated by tier. Tiger-safe slang (e.g., *"crush it"*, *"on point"*) is wrong for a Mouse in Week 2 — it signals a context the student can't yet inhabit. Slang is presented as an aspirational unlock, introduced with: *"When you're Tiger, you'll use this."*

---

## The 3-Game Cognitive Architecture

**Competition Game** — Adrenaline is a memory encoder. When students compete to use vocabulary correctly under time pressure, cortisol + dopamine tag the words as important. Retention increases 40% vs passive delivery.

**Collaboration Game** — Social proof. When a student hears their peer use a word correctly, it normalizes usage. The group becomes the permission structure.

**Creation Game** — Student-generated content creates the deepest ownership. When a student invents a sentence using this week's vocabulary, the word becomes theirs.

---

## The 60-Minute Lesson Breakdown

| Phase | Duration | Content |
|-------|----------|---------|
| Warm-up | 5 min | Review last week's Tiger words |
| Vocabulary delivery | 15 min | 10 words × macro/specific/micro |
| Idiom injection | 10 min | 5 idioms with story anchors |
| Slang contextualization | 5 min | 5 slang terms, tier-gated |
| Game 1 (Competition) | 8 min | Speed round, vocab under pressure |
| Game 2 (Collaboration) | 8 min | Team task using this week's language |
| Game 3 (Creation) | 7 min | Student-generated content |
| Close + micro-assessment | 2 min | Teacher logs behaviors |

---

## The Cut Protocol — Time Overruns

If time is short, cut in this order:
1. Reduce slang from 5 to 3
2. Shorten Game 3 (Creation) to 4 minutes
3. Reduce Warm-up to 2 minutes

Never cut vocabulary. Never cut Game 1. Core physics must run.
