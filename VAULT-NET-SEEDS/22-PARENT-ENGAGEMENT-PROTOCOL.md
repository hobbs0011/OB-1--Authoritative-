## Why Parent Engagement Matters in Vietnam

In Vietnamese family culture, parents are not passive observers of education — they are the economic decision-makers, the emotional stakeholders, and the primary word-of-mouth channel. A child who advances from Mouse to Tiger does not renew their enrollment. Their parent does. Winning the parent is winning the retention.

Vietnamese family hierarchy means parents command significant influence over a student's learning attitude. A parent who believes in NET reinforces the student at home. A skeptical parent undermines progress regardless of lesson quality.

## The Weekly Parent Update Format

Every Friday, parents receive a structured update via Zalo. Four components:

1. **Progress Summary** — 2-3 sentences on this week's content and how their child engaged.
2. **Tiger Meter Score** — A 0-10 behavioral rating (see below).
3. **Next Week Preview** — One sentence on upcoming theme so parents can prime the conversation at home.
4. **Parent Action Item** — One specific thing parents can do this week to reinforce learning (e.g., "Ask your child to teach you today's three idioms at dinner").

Updates are templated and auto-generated by SyncCore, then reviewed by the teacher before sending.

## The Tiger Meter

A 0-10 behavioral scale with visible anchors:

- **0–2 (Mouse Zone)**: Silent in class, translating before speaking, avoiding eye contact with questions.
- **3–5 (Cub Zone)**: Attempting responses, making errors but not shutting down, laughing at mistakes.
- **6–8 (Rising Tiger)**: Initiating in English without prompting, helping peers, showing confidence in games.
- **9–10 (Tiger)**: Leading discussions, owning the room in English, using idioms naturally.

Parents understand this scale intuitively. They see it move. Movement creates retention.

## Handling Parent Concerns About AI Teaching

The 3-Objection Response Framework:

1. **"Is AI really teaching my child?"** → *"CHATTY PRIME handles repetition and practice. Michael and our teachers handle breakthroughs. AI never replaces human judgment — it amplifies it."*
2. **"How do I know it's working?"** → *"The Tiger Meter shows you weekly. The monthly assessment shows you in detail. Progress is visible, not promised."*
3. **"What if my child hates talking to a machine?"** → *"Most students forget it's AI within 10 minutes. The games make it feel like a classroom, not a computer."*

## The Parent Community

Zalo groups segmented by student tier:
- **Mice Parents** — Support group, beginner tips, encouragement culture.
- **Tiger Parents** — Celebration channel, advanced content previews, referral discussions.

Parents in these groups support each other. This is social proof that compounds without additional marketing spend.

## Parents as Evangelists

The parent success story capture protocol:
1. When a student achieves a tier advancement, contact the parent within 24 hours.
2. Ask one question: *"What difference have you noticed at home?"*
3. Record the response verbatim (with permission).
4. Format as a one-paragraph story with first name and tier.
5. Share in Zalo groups and on NET social presence.

## Parent Involvement Limits

Parents may: ask questions, request progress updates, attend one open observation session per quarter. Parents may not: request curriculum changes, demand specific teacher assignments, override tier placement decisions. These boundaries are stated clearly at enrollment and reinforced politely when tested.

---

## The Difficult Parent Protocol

When parent demands conflict with TMST standards: listen fully, validate the concern, then hold the standard. *"We hear you, and our method is designed precisely to protect your child's progress. Changing the approach would risk the results you're paying for."* Escalate to Michael only if the conflict threatens the student's enrollment.

## Monthly Parent Briefings

15-minute one-on-one Zalo call or voice note review, once per month. Agenda: Tiger Meter trend, one win to celebrate, one growth edge, one parent action for next month. Keeps parents invested without overwhelming the teacher's schedule.
