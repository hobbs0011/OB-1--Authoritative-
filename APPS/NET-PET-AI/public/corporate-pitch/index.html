The real problem

It is not vocabulary. It is not grammar. It is what happened to you before you ever walked into a classroom.

“The real problem is something crammed into your head and heart ever since you were taught how to write and speak Vietnamese: mistakes are bad, and the person who made the mistake is bad. The fear of making mistakes, losing face — that is your biggest enemy.”

Most programs teach grammar. You already know grammar. You freeze because you have never had a safe place to speak every day. The method below is built to fix that — not to add more rules, but to remove the barrier before you even open your mouth.

The method

Six principles, a daily habit, and three non-negotiable class rules. No textbook drills. No shame.

🧠

KISS

Keep it simple, stupid. Broken English that communicates beats perfect grammar that freezes. Get to the point. Directly. Even if it is broken.

🧩

Two brains

Your Vietnamese brain is strong. Your English brain is the one you are trying to build. If you bridge them, you handicap yourself. You cannot learn English while thinking in Vietnamese.

🎯

Mistakes are guideposts

Mistakes are good because they show you: this is not the right way. Then this must be. Laugh at yourself. Do not take yourself so seriously.

📖

Bruce Lee vocabulary

It is not about knowing 10,000 words. It is about knowing 500–1,000 words and using each in 10 situations. That makes you a dynamic speaker.

📺

60% comprehensible input

Listen to material you understand at least 60% of. Below that, switch to easier content or English subtitles. Vietnamese subtitles are forbidden — same two-brain problem.

🗣️

WWWWH: keep it going

Ask Who, What, Where, When, How, Why — especially Why. When you find why someone does something, you can talk for hours. The weakest skill is not answering; it is not following up.

The three class rules

1. No Vietnamese — not even one word. If you are thinking in Vietnamese, you are freezing. 90 minutes. English only.

2. No notebooks, no scripts. If you wrote it down, you are not listening. It is a crutch.

3. No phones. Translation apps and TikTok kill active listening. Leave it outside. The only person allowed to translate is the teacher, and only after 3 attempts from different angles.

Daily habit: 45/15

45 minutes of listening to content you enjoy — YouTube, podcasts, TV — plus 15 minutes speaking. No extra commute. No textbook. Just habit.

You can do it while you cook, while you drive, while you put on makeup. The system is designed to take zero extra time from your schedule.
