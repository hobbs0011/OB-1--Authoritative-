Forever Foundations

Core principles — not memorization, acquisition

The Foundation

This is not feedback for Session 1. These are foundational pillars of how to acquire a language naturally — not superficial methods that dominate Vietnamese ESL.

Do not rush. These are fundamentals. If you dig your foundations deep, English will be acquired as part of you.

Two Brains, No Mixing

You have a Vietnamese brain and an English brain. They do not mix. If your right arm is weaker than your left and you always use your strong arm to "help" the weak one, the weak arm never improves. Same rule here.

Rule #1 — Two brains, no mixing. No Vietnamese — speaking, thinking, reading, or writing — during class or home study. No exceptions.

The Answer Formula

Rule #2 — Answer + one detail + ask back. "Yes" is not an answer. "Yes, I like street food because it's cheap" is an answer. Then ask: "What about you?"

Active Listening

Active listening is not just words. It is tone of voice, what is not being said, facial expressions, hand gestures, all non-verbal communication, and situational context.

Rule #3 — Listen to what you know. You focus on the one word you missed and miss the whole point. If you understand 60%, keep going.

Daily Input

Rule #4 — One hour of English input every day. Week 1: 20 min, builds to 60. English subtitles only. Vietnamese subtitles are forbidden. Notebooks banned during this time.

Listening Lab

Train your ear to catch what matters

The "How the heck are you" Test

Canadian speed: "How the heck are you?" Your brain should focus on what you do know — "how... are you" — and treat "heck" as slang noise.

The mistake: 80–90% of Vietnamese learners hear only "THE HECK" and panic. They miss the one thing they actually understood.

20% Comprehension is Enough

When you master this skill, you can completely miss individual words and still grasp the overall meaning. You can piece together sentences from the parts you know, body language, and context.

The power: With as little as 20–30% comprehension, you can understand the full picture.

Daily Listening Rules

  • English subtitles only. Vietnamese subtitles activate your Vietnamese brain. That is the opposite of what you are here to do.
  • 60% comprehension minimum. Below 60%? Switch to easier content. Above 80%? Level up.
  • No notebooks. You are building comprehension, not a vocabulary list. Let context carry meaning.
  • Keep moving. If you hear a word 4–5 times across different situations, that is a useful word. Once? Let it go.

Speaking Practice

Open questions → listen → follow up → repeat

The Loop That Works

Open question → listen → follow up → listen → follow up. This is the cycle. Every answer contains at least three more questions. The most powerful word in English is Why.

WWWWH — Your Follow-Up Weapon

Who, What, Where, When, How, Why. Every answer contains at least three more questions. When you find the why, you have found their passion. And when you have found their passion, you can talk for hours.

  • Closed: "Do you like food?" → "Yes." End of conversation.
  • Open: "What is your favorite food and why?" → A world of follow-ups.
  • Scripts = stagnant. You are waiting for your turn to deliver what you wrote earlier, instead of following the conversation where it goes.

The Fear

The most common fear: "I will run out of questions." The solution is not a longer list. The solution is to stop being afraid of running out of questions.

Everybody's favorite topic is themselves. Even people who seem selfless — they are selfless because it makes them feel good. This is not selfishness. This is life.

6 Rules — Next Class

  1. No Vietnamese. Even one word means your Vietnamese brain took over. 90 minutes. English only. If you don't know a word, describe it in English. Three different ways. Translation is last resort.
  2. No notebooks, no scripts. If you wrote questions before class, you won't hear the answers during class. Throw it away. You are leaning on a crutch and not learning to think on your feet.
  3. No phones. Phones are banned. Translation apps = brain rot. TikTok = attention death. The only person allowed to use translate is the teacher. And he will rarely use it.
  4. Answer + add one detail + ask back. "Yes" is not an answer. "Yes, I like street food because it's cheap and you can eat anywhere" is an answer. Then ask: "What about you?"
  5. Listen to what you know, not what you don't. Vietnamese students focus on the one word they don't know and miss the whole meaning. Context carries meaning. If you understand 60%, keep going. Don't freeze.
