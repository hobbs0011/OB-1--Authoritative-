## OVERVIEW

Assessment in the Phoenix system is not a test. It is a structured insight event — a moment where the student, the teacher, and the parent all gain clarity on where the student stands and what comes next. Assessment creates accountability, drives curriculum, generates parent loyalty, and — when designed correctly — is itself a powerful teaching moment. This document codifies the complete Phoenix assessment architecture from rubrics to review sessions.

> "The assessment is not about the score. It is about the student seeing themselves clearly — and believing the reflection." — Agent-100

---

## THE MONTHLY ASSESSMENT FORMAT

Assessments are conducted at weeks 3, 6, 9, and 12 of each semester. Format is always:

1. **Student self-prediction** (5 minutes): Student rates themselves 1–10 on each of the 5 competencies before the assessment begins
2. **Competency assessment** (25 minutes): Structured evaluation across all 5 competencies
3. **Score reveal and comparison** (10 minutes): Student's prediction vs. actual score — the learning moment
4. **Growth delta review** (5 minutes): Current score vs. last assessment — what improved, what didn't, and why
5. **Next milestone setting** (5 minutes): Student commits to one specific improvement by next assessment

Total time: 50 minutes. The assessment session IS the lesson for that day.

## THE FIVE COMPETENCIES

### Competency 1: VOCABULARY RETENTION

**What it measures**: The percentage of the semester's target vocabulary that the student can produce correctly (not just recognize) in spontaneous speech.

**Scoring method**: Teacher provides a context or scenario. Student must produce target vocabulary without prompting. Score based on accuracy and spontaneity.

| Score | Behavioral Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Fewer than 20% of target words produced; requires significant prompting |
| 3–4 | 20–40% of target words produced; some prompting required |
| 5–6 | 40–60% produced spontaneously with occasional errors |
| 7–8 | 60–80% produced spontaneously and correctly |
| 9–10 | 80–100% produced spontaneously, correctly, and in new contexts |

---

### Competency 2: IDIOM USAGE IN CONTEXT

**What it measures**: Whether the student can deploy semester idioms in contextually appropriate situations — not just define them.

**Scoring method**: Teacher presents 5 scenarios. Student must select and use the appropriate idiom naturally.

| Score | Behavioral Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Cannot recall or apply idioms; confuses meaning |
| 3–4 | Recalls idioms but applies them incorrectly or robotically |
| 5–6 | Applies 3–4 idioms correctly; delivery sounds rehearsed |
| 7–8 | Applies idioms in appropriate contexts; delivery natural |
| 9–10 | Uses idioms spontaneously, creatively, and accurately in new situations |

---

### Competency 3: SPEAKING FLUENCY

**What it measures**: The student's ability to maintain continuous, intelligible English speech for a sustained period without excessive pausing, code-switching, or communication breakdown.

**Scoring method**: 2-minute unscripted monologue on a topic announced 1 minute before. Scored on continuity, vocabulary diversity, and communicative success.

| Score | Behavioral Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Frequent stops, switches to Vietnamese, cannot complete sentences |
| 3–4 | Speaks with significant gaps; basic sentences only |
| 5–6 | Maintains speech with some hesitation; limited vocabulary range |
| 7–8 | Continuous speech with minor hesitations; good vocabulary range |
| 9–10 | Fluent, varied, and confident; near-spontaneous production |

---

### Competency 4: PRONUNCIATION ACCURACY

**What it measures**: Intelligibility — whether a listener unfamiliar with Vietnamese-accented English can understand the student without significant effort.

**Scoring method**: Student reads 5 targeted sentences with known phoneme challenges. Teacher scores on intelligibility, not "native accent" achievement.

| Score | Behavioral Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Frequent phoneme errors that cause comprehension failure |
| 3–4 | Errors present but communication survives; listener works hard |
| 5–6 | Mostly intelligible; 2–3 persistent phoneme issues |
| 7–8 | Clearly intelligible; minor accent features that don't impede understanding |
| 9–10 | Fully intelligible in professional context at natural speaking speed |

---

### Competency 5: CONFIDENCE INDEX

**What it measures**: The student's willingness to attempt English communication under uncertainty — the most important predictor of long-term fluency.

**Scoring method**: Behavioral observation across the assessment session. How does the student respond to challenge, error, and unfamiliar vocabulary?

| Score | Behavioral Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Refuses to attempt unknown items; shuts down under pressure |
| 3–4 | Attempts with visible anxiety; errors cause significant disengagement |
| 5–6 | Attempts most items; recovers from errors with encouragement |
| 7–8 | Attempts all items; self-corrects; maintains engagement after errors |
| 9–10 | Actively seeks challenge; treats errors as information; independent momentum |

## ASSESSMENT AS A TEACHING MOMENT

The self-prediction step is not administrative — it is the most important teaching moment of the assessment. When students predict their own scores first:

- They must think metacognitively about their own competencies
- The comparison with actual scores reveals blind spots (overconfidence or underconfidence)
- Students who consistently underestimate themselves are shown the evidence that they are better than they believe
- Students who overestimate themselves are not shamed — they are given a clear map to reach the score they predicted

> "The student who thinks they deserve a 4 and scores an 8 has just had the most important class of their year." — Agent-100

## THE PARENT REVIEW SESSION

Following each assessment, a 15-minute parent briefing is conducted (Zalo or in-person). Format:

1. Share the Tiger Meter with current scores
2. Highlight one strong competency with a specific example
3. Identify one growth competency with the specific plan to improve it
4. Show the progression delta from last assessment
5. Confirm the next milestone and ask the parent to support one specific homework habit at home

Parent review sessions convert parents from observers to active co-investors in their child's progress.

## ASSESSMENT DATA FEEDS CURRICULUM

Assessment scores are not filed and forgotten. They directly drive the following session's content:

| Assessment Finding | Curriculum Response |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary Retention below 5 | Reduce new vocabulary next 3 sessions; reinforce known words |
| Idiom Usage below 4 | Add 2 additional idiom games per session |
| Fluency below 5 | Increase timed speaking tasks; reduce written exercises |
| Pronunciation below 4 | Add weekly pronunciation clinic; assign phoneme homework |
| Confidence below 4 | Reduce group pressure; increase 1-on-1 wins; change game format |

## ASSESSMENT RUBRICS BY LEVEL

### Mouse Level Assessment
Focus: Survival vocabulary, basic sentence production, fundamental pronunciation.
Scale weight: Vocabulary 40%, Confidence 30%, Pronunciation 20%, Fluency 10%, Idioms 0% (not yet assessed).

### Tiger Level Assessment
Focus: Spontaneous production, professional vocabulary, idiom mastery, prosody.
Scale weight: Fluency 30%, Idioms 25%, Vocabulary 20%, Confidence 15%, Pronunciation 10%.

## THE ASSESSMENT AS A PRODUCT

The assessment report is not just a teaching tool — it is a product that parents pay for through enrollment. When delivered with professional presentation, Tiger Meter visual, specific examples, and parent briefing, it creates perceived value that exceeds the lesson itself.

Families who receive a well-constructed assessment report are statistically less likely to cancel during the semester — even when progress is slower than expected. The report proves the system is watching, measuring, and caring.
