## 1. Core Philosophy — Why We Exist

> *"Our goal is to graduate our students. Most English centers are built around keeping you a student for as long as possible to make as much money from you as possible. We have a different mindset — we want you to graduate after a year."* — Michael Hobbs

Natural English Training exists to **acquire** language, not just learn it. Our methods are based on how humans learned their first language — through comprehensible input, observation, repetition, and play. We reject the Vietnamese standard of memorizing large volumes of useless words with no repetition and no scientific spacing.

### The Three Mistakes of Traditional Vietnamese English Education

| Mistake | What They Do | What NET Does Instead |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Useless Vocabulary** | Memorize large amounts of words that aren't useful in everyday life | Focus only on high-frequency, practical words students will actually use |
| **2. No Repetition** | Learn a word once and move on | Systematic review through AI-powered spaced repetition |
| **3. No Scientific Spacing** | Random review with no strategy | University-proven spaced repetition intervals that defeat the forgetting curve |

### The NET Mindset

- **Acquire, don't just learn.** Mirror how we learned our first language.
- **Graduate, don't retain.** Set students up with tools to keep progressing independently after one year.
- **Quality over quantity.** Know fewer words deeply — use them in many ways — that proves true comprehension.
- **Daily commitment is non-negotiable.** 1 hour of English every day. This is not a magic system.
- **Fun is not optional — it's science.** Studies prove the brain learns faster when engaged and having fun. Play is how animals prepare for real life. Play is how humans acquire language.
- **Passion drives acquisition.** Connect English to what students already love. An 8-year-old who couldn't speak any English was speaking full sentences after one year — because the lessons focused on chess, Free Fire, and Roblox. He talked non-stop because he *wanted* to.

## 2. The Four Pillars of NET Pedagogy

### Pillar 1 — Learn Like a Native

Natural acquisition over grammar drilling. We prioritize listening and mimicking over rote memorization. Speaking, pronunciation, grammar, reading, and writing develop **organically** — sound and meaning before rules.

- Based on Krashen's i+1 theory (comprehensible input hypothesis)
- 60% comprehension threshold — the sweet spot
- Context-based learning
- Minimal explicit grammar instruction
- Immersion methodology

### Pillar 2 — Comprehensible Input (The 60% Rule)

A student must understand **at least 60%** of what they're listening to for information to stick.

| Comprehension Level | Result |
|---|---|
| Below 60% | Student is lost — anxiety zone, no acquisition |
| **60–80% (Sweet Spot)** | **Natural acquisition happens — brain fills gaps from context** |
| Above 90% | Too easy — no growth, brain disengages |

**Daily practice:** 45 minutes listening to content the student **enjoys**  
**Subtitle strategy:** Vietnamese → English → None (progressive removal)  
**Key rule:** Focus on context, not individual words

### Pillar 3 — Jolly Phonics (44 Sounds)

We use the proven Jolly Phonics system developed in England — a simplified, systematic approach to the 44 sounds in English. A big part of NET PET AI will focus on building strong foundations in these sounds and correcting bad habits.

**The 7 Sound Groups:**

| Group | Sounds |
|---|---|
| 1 | s, a, t, i, p, n |
| 2 | c/k, e, h, r, m, d |
| 3 | g, o, u, l, f, b |
| 4 | ai, j, oa, ie, ee, or |
| 5 | z, w, ng, v, oo (long/short), y |
| 6 | x, ch, sh, th (voiced/unvoiced), qu |
| 7 | ou, oi, ue, er, ar |

**Common Vietnamese Speaker Issues:**
- /th/ sounds (THis, THat, THink)
- /r/ vs /l/ confusion
- /v/ vs /w/ confusion
- Final consonant deletion
- Past tense -ed pronunciation

### Pillar 4 — Spaced Repetition

The cornerstone of the AI-PET system. Scientifically optimized intervals interrupt the forgetting curve and embed knowledge into long-term memory.

**The Interval Sequence:**  
Day 0 → Day 1 → Day 3 → Day 7 → Week 2 → Month 1 → Month 3 → **Permanent**

> *"You will repeat and be reminded of new words often — maybe every day for a week or two, then a couple times a week, then once a month, then once every 3 months, and then it is permanently your word."* — Michael Hobbs

**AI-PET Integration:** Students log their "Key Struggle Points" daily. The AI learns what to review and re-introduces concepts at optimal intervals.

## 3. The Mouse → Tiger Transformation

The central metaphor and behavioral framework of NET. Every student enters as a Mouse and graduates as a Tiger.

### Mouse Voice (Before NET)

| Behavior | Description |
|---|---|
| Quiet | Mumbles, trails off mid-sentence |
| Apologetic | "Sorry for my bad English" — constantly |
| Translating | Mentally converts Vietnamese → English before speaking |
| Freezing | Shuts down with native speakers |
| Grammar-obsessed | Studies rules endlessly, never speaks |
| Perfectionist | Won't speak until "perfect" — so never speaks |
| Avoids eye contact | Self-deprecating body language |

### Tiger Voice (After NET)

| Behavior | Description |
|---|---|
| Loud & clear | Projects voice with confidence |
| Unapologetic | Makes mistakes without apology — keeps going |
| Thinks in English | No mental translation — direct expression |
| Approaches confidently | Initiates conversation with foreigners |
| Speaking-first | Practices output before studying rules |
| Growth mindset | Mistakes are data, not failure |
| Strong eye contact | Self-assured, present |

### The Transformation Process

| Phase | Timeline | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Awareness** | Week 1–2 | Identify Mouse behaviors — student learns to recognize them |
| **2. Practice** | Week 3–6 | Call out Mouse, praise Tiger — active behavioral coaching |
| **3. Integration** | Week 7–12 | Raise stakes, celebrate milestones — Tiger becomes habitual |
| **4. Mastery** | Month 4+ | Tiger is the default — Mouse is gone |

### Teacher Intervention Scripts

- *"You're using Mouse Voice right now"* — awareness trigger
- *"Speak louder"* — force projection
- *"No translation"* — break the Vietnamese-first habit
- *"Mistakes are wins"* — reframe errors as learning data

### Assessment Metrics (Tiger Transformation Indicators)

- Volume level (1–10 scale)
- Apology count per class
- Eye contact frequency
- Voluntary participation rate
- Monthly audio sample recordings for comparison

**Success = Tiger Transformation Achieved:**
- 7+ volume consistently
- Zero apologies for mistakes
- Volunteers without prompting
- Strong eye contact with teacher and peers
- Completes "Tiger Challenge" outside class

## 4. Anti-Shame Pedagogy

**Core Principle:** Confidence first, accuracy second.

A big belief in Vietnam is that you can only learn through suffering. This is false. Studies prove a person learns faster when the brain is having fun and engaged.

### Prohibited Language

Never say: *"Wrong," "Bad," "Stupid," "Failure"*

### Required Language

Always say: *"Shift," "Calibrate," "Upgrade," "Refine," "Try again," "Good effort"*

### Feedback Protocol — The Sandwich Method

1. **Praise:** "Great job speaking for 30 seconds!"
2. **Correction:** "Let's calibrate this — 'I go' should be 'I went'"
3. **Praise:** "Your confidence is excellent!"

### Emotional Safety Rules

- Create a safe space where mistakes are not just allowed but **encouraged**
- Celebrate errors as learning data
- Never compare students negatively
- Make English **fun**, not a chore
- Build community support

### Student Psychology — The Three Metaphors

**The Elephant in Chains:** Vietnamese students are told "Don't speak until perfect." Invisible chains hold them back. They have the ability but believe they can't. NET breaks the chains.

**The Grammar Prison:** Traditional education locks students in rules, shames mistakes, forces perfection before speaking. NET is the escape key.

**The Phoenix Rebirth:** Learning English is a rebirth. The old scared self burns away. A new confident speaker emerges. The transformation is permanent.

## 5. The Confidence Algorithm

### The 5-Step Loop

1. **Success Loop** — Easy wins build momentum
2. **Sound Priming** — Activation sentences as warmup
3. **Momentum** — Keep moving, no freezing allowed
4. **Humorous Pressure** — Create emotional memories through laughter
5. **Reward** — Verbal praise, laughter, group support

### Mirroring Technique (Assessment Tool)

- Teacher says: *"I went to the park"*
- Student repeats **with confidence** — focus on projection and clarity
- No translation allowed

### Energy Management

- Read the room constantly
- High energy → competitive games
- Low energy → storytelling and listening
- Adjust pace and activities in real time

## 6. The 10-5-5-3 Lesson Structure

**The mandatory framework for every NET lesson:**

| Component | Count | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Vocabulary** | 10 | Core words with Vietnamese translations, pronunciation guide, definitions, real-world example sentences |
| **Idioms** | 5 | Professional and cultural phrases with context |
| **Slang** | 5 | Modern workplace and Gen Z expressions |
| **Games** | 3 | Warmup → Mid-lesson → Final Boss (interactive, scored) |

### Vocabulary Requirements

- EN/VN toggle for each word
- Pronunciation guide (IPA or Jolly Phonics notation)
- Clear, concise definition
- Real-world example sentence in context
- Context-rich presentation (no isolated word lists)

### Game Design Rules

- **Game 1:** Warmup — vocabulary matching, team competition, high energy
- **Game 2:** Mid-lesson — comprehension check, short answer format, individual or team
- **Game 3:** Final Boss — hardest challenge, integrates all content, scoring system
- All games must be **interactive**, not passive
- Short answer format preferred over multiple choice
- Every game includes a scoring system

## 8. Classroom Techniques

### Language Ratios

- **Beginners:** 70% English / 30% Vietnamese
- **Intermediate+:** 90% English / 10% Vietnamese
- Use visuals, gestures, and context to bridge gaps

### Mimic-First Approach

1. Listen to a phrase (intonation, rhythm, emotion)
2. Repeat exactly — don't analyze grammar
3. Build muscle memory through repetition
4. Grammar understanding follows naturally

### Choral Repetition

- Entire class repeats together — builds confidence through group safety
- Tiger energy encouraged — volume up, no mumbling

### Pair Practice

- Students drill in pairs — peer support and accountability
- Low-stakes environment before full-class performance

### Pronunciation Drills

- Visual cards with sound symbols
- 10–15 repetitions per sound
- Minimal pairs practice (R vs L, V vs W, sheep vs ship)
- Word stress exercises
- Sentence rhythm training

## 13. Technical Implementation (Lesson HTML)

### Requirements

- Single-file HTML (no external dependencies)
- Vanilla JavaScript only (no frameworks)
- `addEventListener` only (no inline `onclick`)
- Gospel 444 compliant CSS
- Mobile-responsive, touch-friendly (48×48px minimum targets)

- Interactive games with scoring system

- Glassmorphism effects (`backdrop-filter: blur(12px)` minimum)

- Animated organic blobs

- Readable font sizes (16px+ body, 20px+ headers)

## 16. The Listening Trap vs. Flow of Context

A key teaching concept for all NET lessons:

**The Listening Trap (Bad):**  
Hear unknown word → **STOP LISTENING** → Focus on unknown → Miss the entire message

**The Flow of Context (Good):**  
Hear unknown word → **KEEP LISTENING** → Use context clues → Understand the message

95% of words in any conversation are known. The 5% unknown words should never stop the flow.
