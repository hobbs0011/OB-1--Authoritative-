## CLASSROOM MANAGEMENT PROTOCOLS (The Playbook)

### The Notebook Mandate
- Policy: "Notebooks out, phones away" before any lesson starts
- Enforcement: Students who don't comply get called out individually
- Escalation: Note check in 10–15 minutes to verify compliance

### The "Free Time" Contract (The Deal)
- Structure: Finish English notes = second period free for relaxation/private study
- Enforcement: Teacher announces deal at start of class
- "You get the rest of the class off if you finish your notes"

### The Snowman Rule (Freeze Drills)
- Mechanism: 10-second countdown for responses
- Failure penalty: TikTok dance or karaoke performance
- Application: Used for vocabulary checks and spontaneous conversation ("Mr. Hoodie" #32 participated)

### "Miss Phone Phone" Intervention
- Strategy: Identify chronic phone users by number/nickname
- Redirect: Assign board-writing tasks to "keep hands busy"
- Example: Student #39 across 12A4, 12A6, and 10A11 assigned to write numbers 1-10 on board

### Mug Shot Threats
- Tactic: Threatening "jail-style" photos of disruptive students to send to parents
- Used in: 10A10, 10A11, general class management
- Context: "Brain rot" behavior — gaming instead of studying

### Tổ Trưởng (Class Leader) Mobilization
- Strategy: Call class leaders to front of room
- Purpose: Make them "part of the solution" instead of allowing them to blend in
- Application: 10A11 specifically

### Randomized Accountability
- Tool: AI generates random student numbers (1-40)
- Effect: No student can hide in the back — anyone could be called
- Purpose: "Active readiness" state across entire class

### The "Mouse to Tiger" Volume Shift
- Identifying students who speak "like a mouse" (e.g., Nguyen Hong Duyen #06)
- Core teaching philosophy: Permission over perfection. Say it loud, even if wrong.
- "When you get angry, be a tiger, not a mouse"

## VOCABULARY FRAMEWORK

### The 10-Word Accountability List
Used across 10A11, 12A4, 12A5, 12A6:

| Word | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| Excuses | Reasons provided to justify behavior |
| Blame | Assigning responsibility for a mistake |
| Justify | Prove to be right |
| Consequence | Result of an action |
| Discipline | Control over behavior |
| Responsibility | A duty or obligation |
| Cooperate | Work together for a common purpose |
| Compromise | Meet halfway / Settlement of a dispute |
| Expectation | A strong belief about the future |
| Progress | Forward movement or advancement |

### Additional Upper-Intermediate Words
- Analyze, Context, Perspective, Evaluate, Implication
- Selection criteria: "Difficult but conversational" — words students will actually use

### Teaching Methods
- **Five-Word Definition Rule:** Students provide concise 5-word English definitions
- **Contextual Usage:** Use two related words in a single sentence (e.g., "responsibility" + "blame")
- **Homonym Check:** "Excuse me" (polite) vs. "Excuses" (justifications)
- **Phonics Drills:** "She sells seashells by the seashore" — tests 's' and 'sh' clarity
- **Humor Bridge:** "Why was the math book sad? Too many problems" — lowers affective filter
- **"Maybe" Ban:** Banned filler word for Justin (#37) — forces "perhaps," "possibly," dynamic alternatives
- **Vietnamese Translation Bridge:** "Tu tin" = confidence. AI translates complex rules into Vietnamese

## STRATEGIC LAZINESS PHILOSOPHY (Private Session — Quan & Quang)

- **Dumb Laziness:** Doing nothing — leads to stress and poverty later

### The Sinking Ship & Life Rafts

## AI AS TEACHING ASSISTANT — WORKFLOW PATTERNS

### What Works
1. **Bilingual translation** — Complex rules and vocabulary definitions into Vietnamese
2. **Random number generation** — Keeps class in "active readiness" state
3. **Roster data entry** — AI "locks in" team rosters for future retrieval
4. **Confidence reports** — Verbal feedback after oral assessments (3 suggestions per student)
5. **Joke generation** — Lowers affective filter before heavy vocabulary
6. **Role-play scenarios** — "Town Police" etc. for interactive vocabulary

### What Fails
1. **Context awareness** — Can't distinguish teacher-to-student vs teacher-to-AI speech
2. **Pronunciation assessment** — "Horrible judge of pronunciation"
3. **Guardrail conflicts** — Refuses to critique student volume due to "guidelines"
4. **Language stability** — Spontaneously switches to Thai, Korean, Chinese
5. **Translation accuracy** — Doesn't translate when commanded, or translates wrong content
6. **Emotion simulation** — Inconsistent when asked to simulate disappointment/sadness

### The "Stupid AI" Pedagogy
- Teacher openly criticizes AI in front of students
- Purpose: Show students that "smart" machines can fail
- Effect: Humanizes teacher, lowers student anxiety about their own errors
- Meta-lesson: Don't trust AI blindly — verify everything

## KEY TEACHING PRINCIPLES DEMONSTRATED

### Mouse to Tiger (Core NET Philosophy)
- Students start as "mice" — quiet, uncertain, afraid to make mistakes
- Goal: Transform them into "tigers" — confident, loud, willing to be wrong
- "Permission over perfection" — say it loud, even if wrong
- "When you get angry, be a tiger, not a mouse"

### The Present Moment
- Students who "future-think" freeze (Pham Nguyen Ngoc Anh)
- Correction: Answer the question NOW. Don't plan the next 5 answers
- "Go as far as you can see, and then you'll see further"

### Accountability over Participation
- "Participation trophies are abolished"
- Progress earned through volume and velocity, never given
- Less than 60% mastery = locked lesson + repeat pressure games
- Greater than 90% = tier jump (Mouse to Cub to Tiger)

### The "Note Check" Economy
- Currency: completed English notes
- Reward: free time in second period
- Enforcement: random checks at 10-15 minute mark
- Escalation: mug shots to parents for non-compliance
