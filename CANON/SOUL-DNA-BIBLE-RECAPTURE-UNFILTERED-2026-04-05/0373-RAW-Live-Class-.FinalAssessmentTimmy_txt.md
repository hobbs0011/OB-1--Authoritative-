The "Translation Trap": Research shows Duolingo relies heavily on the Grammar-Translation method. It teaches you to map Word A (English) to Word B (Native Language). It does not teach you to "think" in English.

The "Context" Problem: The app often uses nonsensical sentences ("The horse eats the rice") to test grammar. This helps with memorizing rules, but students often fail to produce real conversation because they haven't learned the "social" aspect of the language (pragmatics).

Retention vs. Recognition: Duolingo is excellent for passive recognition (seeing a word and knowing it). It is statistically weaker for active production (creating a new sentence from scratch).

Your Advice to Timmy: "Duolingo is great for keeping your vocabulary fresh, but it is a game, not a teacher. If you only use the app, you will get good at the game, not the language. You must pair it with watching real English videos or writing a simple diary."
