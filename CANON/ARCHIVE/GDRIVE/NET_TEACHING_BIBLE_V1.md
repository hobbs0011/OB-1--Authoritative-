# 1. THE FOUR PILLARS: FOUNDATION OF NET

## 1.1 OVERVIEW

The Four Pillars are the **immutable core** of NET's teaching methodology. Every lesson, every interaction, every pedagogical decision must align with these principles.

### WHY FOUR PILLARS?

Traditional Vietnamese English education fails because it violates natural language acquisition. Students study grammar rules for years but can't speak because they:
- Never listen enough (input deficit)
- Rarely practice speaking (output deficit)
- Learn words in isolation (no context)
- Fear mistakes (confidence deficit)

**NET reverses this entirely.**

---

## 1.2 PILLAR 1: LISTEN & UNDERSTAND

### THE PRINCIPLE
**Students must consume massive amounts of comprehensible input.**

Language is acquired, not learned. The brain needs thousands of hours of exposure to patterns, rhythms, intonation, and vocabulary before it can produce fluent speech.

### THE METHOD
**60% Rule:** Content must be 60-80% comprehensible.
- **Below 60%:** Student is lost, frustrated, overwhelmed (Noise wins)
- **60-80%:** Flow state, optimal learning, brain fills gaps naturally
- **Above 90%:** Too easy, no growth, student gets bored

### IMPLEMENTATION

**DAILY PRACTICE (45 minutes minimum):**
- Watch English content (movies, YouTube, podcasts)
- Choose topics student ENJOYS (critical for engagement)
- Use subtitles strategically:
  - Beginners: Vietnamese subtitles okay initially
  - Intermediate: English subtitles preferred
  - Advanced: No subtitles, pure listening

**IN-CLASS PRACTICE:**
- Teacher speaks 70% in English, 30% Vietnamese (beginners)
- Teacher speaks 90% in English, 10% Vietnamese (intermediate+)
- Use visuals, gestures, context to convey meaning
- Repeat key phrases multiple times in different contexts

**CONTENT TYPES:**
- Fun (top priority): Gaming streams, comedy, music, movies
- Interesting: Documentaries, TED talks, interviews
- Useful: Business podcasts, technical videos, news

**CRITICAL RULE:**
**If the student doesn't enjoy the content, they won't listen consistently. Fun beats perfection.**

---

## 1.3 PILLAR 2: SPEAK & MIMIC

### THE PRINCIPLE
**Output is the engine of fluency. Confidence comes from speaking, not studying.**

Students must speak from Day 1, even if it's broken, slow, or grammatically incorrect.

### THE METHOD
**Mimic-First Approach:**
- Listen to a phrase
- Repeat it exactly (intonation, rhythm, emotion)
- Don't analyze grammar — just copy
- Build muscle memory in mouth and brain

### IMPLEMENTATION

**DAILY PRACTICE (15 minutes minimum):**
- Shadow-speak: Play audio, pause, repeat out loud
- Record self speaking
- Compare recording to native speaker
- Focus on sounds, not perfection

**IN-CLASS PRACTICE:**
- **Choral Repetition:** Entire class repeats phrases together
- **Pair Practice:** Students drill phrases in pairs (Tiger energy!)
- **Solo Performance:** Each student speaks individually (confidence building)
- **No Translation:** Students think in English, not translate from Vietnamese

**PRONUNCIATION FOCUS:**
Use the **42 Jolly Phonics Sounds** (see Section 9) to build accurate pronunciation from the beginning.

**CRITICAL RULE:**
**Volume = Confidence. Tiger Voice is loud. Mouse Voice is quiet. Always encourage louder speech.**

---

## 1.4 PILLAR 3: LEARN IN CONTEXT

### THE PRINCIPLE
**Words in isolation are useless. Real sentences stick.**

Traditional education teaches vocabulary lists. Students memorize words but can't use them in conversation. Why? Because the brain doesn't store isolated data — it stores patterns and associations.

### THE METHOD
**Context-First Teaching:**
- Never teach vocabulary in lists
- Always teach words inside real sentences
- Always show multiple example sentences
- Always connect to student's life or interests

### IMPLEMENTATION

**VOCABULARY TEACHING:**
❌ **WRONG:**
> "Sarcasm means... irony or... mocking tone..."
> Word: Sarcasm
> Translation: Mỉa mai

✅ **CORRECT:**
> "Oh great, another meeting. Just what I needed today."
> [Student hears tone, sees eye roll, feels the sarcasm]
> Teacher explains: "This is sarcasm. I don't actually want a meeting. I'm complaining in a funny way."

**EXAMPLE SENTENCES (Show 3-5 per concept):**
1. "Yeah, sure, I'd love to work overtime." (sarcasm = I don't want to)
2. "Thanks a lot for inviting me." (sarcasm = You didn't invite me, I'm annoyed)
3. "Oh, wonderful. Traffic jam." (sarcasm = This is not wonderful)

**ASSOCIATION STRATEGIES:**
- Connect new words to student's hobbies (gaming, tech, food)
- Use memes, movie quotes, viral videos
- Create emotional associations (funny, shocking, relatable)
- Build word families (sarcasm → sarcastic → sarcastically)

**CRITICAL RULE:**
**If a student can't use the word in a real sentence within 5 minutes of learning it, you taught it wrong.**

---

## 1.5 PILLAR 4: BUILD CONFIDENCE

### THE PRINCIPLE
**Fear is the #1 barrier to fluency. Confidence comes before perfection.**

Vietnamese students are trained to avoid mistakes at all costs. This creates **Mouse Voice** — the quiet, scared, apologizing speaker who never improves because they never practice.

**NET reverses this.**

Mistakes are proof of effort. Errors are data. Speaking badly is better than not speaking at all.

### THE METHOD
**Safe Environment:**
- Celebrate mistakes
- Never shame errors
- Praise courage over accuracy
- Create community of support

### IMPLEMENTATION

**IN-CLASS CULTURE:**
- "Great try!" is more important than "Correct!"
- When student makes mistake: "Good! You spoke! Now let's polish it."
- When student is silent: "I need Tiger Voice. Try again, louder."
- When student apologizes: "Tigers don't apologize for learning."

**BUDDY SYSTEM (see Section 8):**
- Pair students for weekly practice
- Encourage peer support
- Build friendships through learning
- Create accountability partners

**COMMUNITY EVENTS (Monthly):**
- Networking dinners in Saigon
- Group activities (karaoke, game nights, debates)
- Alumni talks (show success stories)
- Create sense of belonging

**FEEDBACK PROTOCOLS (see Section 6.2):**
- **Sandwich Method:** Praise → Correction → Praise
- Example:
  1. "Great job speaking for 30 seconds!"
  2. "Let's fix this grammar: 'I go' should be 'I went'"
  3. "Your confidence is excellent. Keep that energy!"

**CRITICAL RULE:**
**Confidence is built through repetition, encouragement, and small wins. Never criticize harshly. Never compare students negatively.**

## 1.6 THE FIVE FORCES (METAPHYSICS OF LEARNING)

These are the invisible dynamics that govern every lesson:

### ENERGY
**Definition:** Student enthusiasm, pace, engagement

**HIGH ENERGY:**
- Students talk over each other (good!)
- Laughter, excitement, movement
- Time flies

**LOW ENERGY:**
- Students silent, checking phones
- Awkward pauses
- Dragging pace

**TEACHER CONTROL:**
- Raise energy: Games, debates, humor
- Lower energy: Reading, writing, reflection

---

### FLOW
**Definition:** Optimal learning state where student is fully immersed

**FLOW INDICATORS:**
- No translation (thinking in English)
- No clock-watching
- Mistakes made without self-consciousness
- Deep focus

**HOW TO CREATE FLOW:**
- Content at 60-80% comprehension
- Clear goals for the session
- Immediate feedback
- Balance of challenge and skill

---

### NOISE
**Definition:** Cognitive overload, the villain of learning

**NOISE HAPPENS WHEN:**
- Too much new information at once
- Grammar rules explained mid-conversation
- Constant interruptions for corrections
- Content too difficult (<60% comprehension)

**HOW TO REDUCE NOISE:**
- Slow down
- Use visuals and gestures
- Simplify sentences
- Focus on meaning, not grammar

---

### CLARITY
**Definition:** Direct understanding without translation

**CLARITY ACHIEVED WHEN:**
- Student understands meaning instantly
- No need to translate to Vietnamese
- Natural comprehension

**HOW TO BUILD CLARITY:**
- Use context clues (pictures, gestures, tone)
- Show, don't explain
- Repeat key phrases in multiple contexts
- Use simple English to explain complex concepts

---

### DRIFT
**Definition:** System deviation from canon

**DRIFT IS THE ENEMY.**

Any lesson, teacher, or content that violates the Four Pillars creates drift. Drift compounds over time and destroys the system.

**ZERO DRIFT PROTOCOL:**
- All lessons audited for Pillar compliance
- Teachers retrained if drift detected
- Content updated to eliminate contradictions

# 2. LESSON ARCHITECTURE: THE 10-5-5-3 SYSTEM

## 2.1 OVERVIEW

Every NET lesson follows the **10-5-5-3 Pattern**:
- **10** Core Vocabulary words
- **5** Idioms
- **5** Slang expressions
- **3** Games (Warm-up, Mid-lesson, Final challenge)

This structure is **non-negotiable** for standard 90-minute classes.

---

## 2.2 THE 90-MINUTE BREAKDOWN

### SEGMENT 1: WARM-UP (10 minutes)
**Goal:** Break ice, activate English brain, raise energy

**Activities:**
- "Weekend Stories" round-robin
- Current mood check-in
- Quick vocab review from last session
- High-energy greeting ritual

**Example:**
> Teacher: "Everyone stand up! How was your weekend? Kiet, start! 30 seconds, GO!"

---

### SEGMENT 2: INPUT (5 minutes)
**Goal:** Present new content (vocabulary, grammar pattern, idiom)

**Method:**
- Show, don't lecture
- Use real examples (movie clips, tweets, memes)
- Keep it SHORT (attention span is limited)

**Example (Teaching Sarcasm):**
> Teacher shows clip from The Office where Jim says, "Oh, that's a great idea" with sarcastic tone.
> Teacher: "Notice his face. Notice his voice. This is sarcasm. He doesn't mean it."

---

### SEGMENT 3: GUIDED PRACTICE (5 minutes)
**Goal:** Students try new content with teacher support

**Method:**
- Choral repetition (entire class repeats together)
- Pair drills (students practice with partners)
- Error correction in real-time (gentle, encouraging)

**Example:**
> Teacher: "Everyone repeat after me: 'Oh great, another meeting.' Try to sound annoyed!"
> Class: "Oh great, another meeting!"
> Teacher: "Good! Now in pairs, create your own sarcastic sentence. 2 minutes, GO!"

---

### SEGMENT 4: FREE PRACTICE (60 minutes)
**Goal:** Students speak freely, apply content in real conversations

**Method:**
- Pair/group conversations
- Role-plays, debates, storytelling
- Teacher circulates, listens, takes notes
- **NO interruption during speech** (critical rule)

**Typical Activities:**
- **Role-play:** "You're at a restaurant. The food is terrible. Use sarcasm to complain politely."
- **Debate:** "Which is better: iPhone or Android? Convince your partner."
- **Storytelling:** "Tell a story about a time you used sarcasm and got in trouble."

**TEACHER ROLE:**
- Listen actively
- Note common errors (write on board for later)
- Praise specific wins ("Great use of 'I'd rather'!")
- Don't interrupt unless student is completely stuck

---

### SEGMENT 5: FEEDBACK (10 minutes)
**Goal:** Correct common errors, reinforce wins, recap lesson

**Method:**
- List 3-5 common mistakes on board
- Explain correct versions
- Praise 3 students for specific achievements
- Quick recap of lesson focus

**Example:**
> Teacher: "Great job today! Three things to fix:
> 1. 'I go yesterday' → 'I went yesterday' (past tense)
> 2. 'He don't like' → 'He doesn't like' (third person)
> 3. 'More better' → 'Better' (no 'more' needed)
>
> Three wins:
> - Kiet: Amazing sarcasm in role-play!
> - Nhan: You spoke louder today, great Tiger energy!
> - Quang: Perfect pronunciation of 'throughout'!
>
> Homework: Watch 1 episode of Friends, count how many times they use sarcasm."

---

## 2.3 THE THREE GAMES MANDATE

Every lesson MUST include **3 games**:

### GAME 1: WARM-UP GAME (First 10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Break ice, raise energy, activate brains

**Examples:**
- **Two Truths, One Lie:** Students share 3 statements, class guesses which is false
- **Would You Rather:** "Would you rather fight 100 duck-sized horses or 1 horse-sized duck?"
- **Hot Seat:** One student faces away from board, class describes word on screen without saying it

---

### GAME 2: MID-LESSON GAME (After 45 minutes)
**Purpose:** Re-energize, apply lesson content, make learning fun

**Examples:**
- **Sarcasm Olympics:** Teams compete to deliver most convincing sarcastic lines
- **Debate Championship:** Pair vs pair, 2-minute arguments, class votes on winner
- **Charades:** Act out idioms, phrases, or scenarios

---

### GAME 3: FINAL CHALLENGE (Last 15 minutes)
**Purpose:** High-stakes practice, demonstrate mastery, end on high note

**Examples:**
- **Mock Interview:** Students interview each other for dream job
- **Shark Tank:** Pitch a ridiculous product to the class
- **Improv Scene:** Teacher gives scenario, students act it out using lesson vocab

**CRITICAL RULE:**
**Games are not filler. Games are where real learning happens. Pressure + Fun = Retention.**

## 3.2 CATEGORY 1: PRAGMATICS (CULTURAL COMMUNICATION)

**Purpose:** Teach cultural nuances that Vietnamese students miss in traditional education

### TOPIC 1: SARCASM
**Lesson Focus:** "Yeah, right" / "Good luck with that" / "Thanks a lot"

**Key Learning:**
- Tone and facial expression matter more than words
- Sarcasm is common in English-speaking cultures
- How to detect sarcasm (context clues)
- How to use sarcasm politely

**Example Sentences:**
- "Oh great, another Zoom meeting." (sarcasm = I don't want this)
- "Sure, I'd love to work on Saturday." (sarcasm = No, I wouldn't)

---

### TOPIC 2: FUNNY VS. FUNNY
**Lesson Focus:** Different types of humor

**Key Learning:**
- "Funny ha-ha" (makes you laugh) vs. "Funny weird" (strange, odd)
- Slapstick vs. dry humor vs. dark humor
- Cultural differences in comedy

**Example Sentences:**
- "That movie was funny!" (ha-ha)
- "That smell is funny..." (weird)

---

### TOPIC 3: UNDERSTATEMENT
**Lesson Focus:** British/American tendency to downplay

**Key Learning:**
- "Not bad" = actually quite good
- "A bit annoying" = extremely annoying
- Why English speakers don't exaggerate emotions

**Example Sentences:**
- "The weather is a bit chilly." (actually freezing)
- "I'm somewhat disappointed." (actually devastated)

---

### TOPIC 4: READING BETWEEN THE LINES
**Lesson Focus:** Indirect communication

**Key Learning:**
- What people mean vs. what they say
- "We should hang out sometime" (often meaningless politeness)
- "Let me think about it" (usually means no)

**Example Sentences:**
- "That's interesting..." (I disagree but won't say it)
- "I'll try my best" (I probably can't do it)

---

### TOPIC 5: VAGUE TIME
**Lesson Focus:** "Soon" / "Later" / "Eventually"

**Key Learning:**
- English time words are imprecise
- "Soon" could mean 5 minutes or 5 days
- Cultural frustration with vague timelines

**Example Sentences:**
- "I'll do it later" (no specific time promised)
- "We'll get there eventually" (could be hours)

---

### TOPIC 6: PASSIVE AGGRESSION
**Lesson Focus:** Indirect anger

**Key Learning:**
- Saying one thing, meaning another (with anger)
- "Fine." (not fine at all)
- How to recognize and respond to passive aggression

**Example Sentences:**
- "No, really, it's fine. Don't worry about it." (angry)
- "Whatever you want." (I don't agree but won't fight)

---

### TOPIC 7: "INTERESTING"
**Lesson Focus:** The most flexible word in English

**Key Learning:**
- "Interesting" = good, bad, or neutral depending on tone
- Positive: "That's an interesting idea!" (I like it)
- Negative: "That's... interesting." (I hate it)

**Example Sentences:**
- "Your approach is interesting" (positive: creative)
- "That's an interesting outfit" (negative: weird)

---

### TOPIC 8: CULTURAL REFERENCES
**Lesson Focus:** Movies, memes, idioms from pop culture

**Key Learning:**
- Why people say "May the Force be with you"
- Common memes ("OK Boomer", "This is fine")
- Understanding references improves listening comprehension

**Example Sentences:**
- "Winter is coming" (Game of Thrones = trouble ahead)
- "I'll be back" (Terminator = I'm leaving but will return)

---

### TOPIC 9: TABOO TOPICS
**Lesson Focus:** What NOT to talk about

**Key Learning:**
- Politics, religion, money = avoid in small talk
- How to politely change subject
- Cultural differences in taboo topics

**Example Sentences:**
- "Let's not talk about politics..."
- "I'd rather not discuss that."

---

### TOPIC 10: COMPLIMENTS & DEFLECTION
**Lesson Focus:** How to accept praise

**Key Learning:**
- Americans say "Thank you" to compliments
- Vietnamese often deflect ("No, I'm not good")
- Practice accepting compliments gracefully

**Example Sentences:**
- ❌ "No, I'm terrible at English"
- ✅ "Thank you! I've been practicing a lot"

---

### TOPIC 11: APOLOGIES (TOO MANY)
**Lesson Focus:** When to say "sorry" and when NOT to

**Key Learning:**
- Vietnamese say "sorry" excessively
- English speakers only apologize for real mistakes
- Over-apologizing signals insecurity

**Example Sentences:**
- ❌ "Sorry for my bad English" (don't apologize for learning!)
- ✅ "I'm still learning English" (confident statement)

---

### TOPIC 12: SMALL TALK MASTERY
**Lesson Focus:** Weather, weekend, sports

**Key Learning:**
- Small talk is ritual, not deep conversation
- Topics: Weather, weekend plans, commute, food
- How to keep conversation light and friendly

**Example Sentences:**
- "How was your weekend?"
- "Crazy weather today, right?"

---

## 3.3 CATEGORY 2: WORKPLACE ENGLISH (PROFESSIONAL)

### TOPIC 13: DAILY STANDUP
**Lesson Focus:** Agile team meetings

**Key Learning:**
- "What I did yesterday / What I'll do today / Blockers"
- Keep it short (1-2 minutes max)
- Speak confidently, no rambling

**Example Script:**
> "Yesterday I finished the login feature. Today I'll work on the dashboard. No blockers."

---

### TOPIC 14: POLITE DISAGREEING
**Lesson Focus:** Saying "no" without offending

**Key Learning:**
- Never say "You're wrong"
- Use softeners: "I see your point, but..."
- Offer alternative perspective

**Example Sentences:**
- ❌ "That's wrong"
- ✅ "I see it differently. What if we..."

---

### TOPIC 15: INTERRUPTING POLITELY
**Lesson Focus:** How to cut in without being rude

**Key Learning:**
- "Sorry to interrupt, but..."
- "Can I add something quickly?"
- Timing matters (don't interrupt mid-sentence)

**Example Sentences:**
- "Sorry to jump in, but I have a concern..."
- "Quick question before we move on..."

---

### TOPIC 16: DELIVERING BAD NEWS
**Lesson Focus:** Softening negative information

**Key Learning:**
- Start with positive (if possible)
- Be direct but empathetic
- Offer solution or next steps

**Example Structure:**
> "The good news is we finished testing. The bad news is we found 10 bugs. Here's the plan to fix them..."

---

### TOPIC 17: ASKING FOR A PROMOTION
**Lesson Focus:** Negotiation skills

**Key Learning:**
- Build case with data (achievements, metrics)
- Ask confidently, not apologetically
- Be prepared for negotiation

**Example Script:**
> "I've increased sales by 30% this year. I've taken on extra projects. I'd like to discuss a promotion."

---

### TOPIC 18: EXPLAINING BUGS
**Lesson Focus:** Technical communication for developers

**Key Learning:**
- Describe symptom (what's wrong)
- Explain expected behavior (what should happen)
- Suggest fix (if known)

**Example:**
> "The login button doesn't respond on iOS. It should redirect to the dashboard. I think it's a CSS issue."

---

### TOPIC 19: CHASING EMAILS
**Lesson Focus:** Following up professionally

**Key Learning:**
- Be polite but persistent
- "Just checking in..."
- "Any updates on this?"

**Example Sentences:**
- "Hi, just circling back on this. Any updates?"
- "Wanted to follow up on my previous email..."

---

### TOPIC 20: GIVING FEEDBACK
**Lesson Focus:** Constructive criticism

**Key Learning:**
- Sandwich method (praise → critique → praise)
- Focus on behavior, not person
- Offer specific suggestions

**Example:**
> "Your design looks great. The colors are a bit too bright — try softer tones. Overall, excellent work!"

---

### TOPIC 21: HANDLING DIFFICULT COWORKERS
**Lesson Focus:** Conflict resolution

**Key Learning:**
- Stay professional, don't take it personally
- Use "I" statements ("I feel..." not "You always...")
- Escalate to manager if needed

**Example:**
- ❌ "You never listen to me!"
- ✅ "I feel like my ideas aren't being heard. Can we discuss?"

---

### TOPIC 22: NETWORKING CONVERSATIONS
**Lesson Focus:** Meeting new people at events

**Key Learning:**
- Elevator pitch (introduce yourself in 30 seconds)
- Ask open-ended questions
- Exchange contact information smoothly

**Example:**
> "Hi, I'm Kiet. I'm a software developer at BlueTech. What brings you here?"

---

### TOPIC 23: PHONE CALLS (BUSINESS)
**Lesson Focus:** Professional phone etiquette

**Key Learning:**
- Greeting: "This is [name] calling from [company]"
- State purpose clearly
- Confirm understanding before hanging up

**Example:**
> "Hi, this is Nhan from BlueTech. I'm calling about the project deadline. Do you have a moment?"

---

### TOPIC 24: PRESENTATIONS
**Lesson Focus:** Public speaking in English

**Key Learning:**
- Start strong (hook audience)
- Signpost structure ("First... Second... Finally...")
- End with clear takeaway

**Example Opening:**
> "Today I'll show you three ways to improve productivity. First, automation. Second, focus time. Third, better meetings."

---

# 4. STUDENT PSYCHOLOGY: MOUSE → TIGER

## 4.1 THE MOUSE ARCHETYPE

### DEFINING CHARACTERISTICS

**MOUSE VOICE:**
- Speaks quietly, mumbles, trails off
- Apologizes constantly ("Sorry for my bad English")
- Translates every sentence from Vietnamese before speaking
- Freezes when addressed by native speakers
- Studies grammar endlessly, speaks rarely
- Perfectionist mindset (won't speak until "ready")
- Avoids eye contact
- Self-deprecating ("I'm so stupid at English")

### PSYCHOLOGICAL ROOTS

Mouse Voice is created by:
1. **Shame-Based Education:** Vietnamese schools punish mistakes harshly
2. **Perfectionism Culture:** "If you can't do it perfectly, don't do it at all"
3. **Face-Saving:** Fear of looking foolish in front of peers
4. **Grammar Prison:** 10+ years of textbook study, zero conversation practice
5. **Authority Fear:** Teacher as judge, not coach

### MOUSE BEHAVIORS IN CLASS

- Sits in back row
- Never volunteers answers
- Looks down when teacher makes eye contact
- Whispers answers to partner instead of speaking aloud
- Over-prepares but underperforms under pressure
- Quits programs within 3 months (low completion rate)

---

## 4.2 THE TIGER ARCHETYPE

### DEFINING CHARACTERISTICS

**TIGER VOICE:**
- Speaks loudly, clearly, with energy
- Makes mistakes without apology
- Thinks in English (minimal translation)
- Approaches native speakers confidently
- Practices speaking before studying grammar
- Growth mindset (mistakes = learning)
- Strong eye contact
- Self-assured ("I'm a learner, getting better every day")

### PSYCHOLOGICAL FOUNDATIONS

Tiger Voice is built through:
1. **Confidence-First Approach:** Speak from Day 1, polish later
2. **Mistake Celebration:** Errors are data, not shame
3. **Supportive Environment:** Teacher as coach, peers as allies
4. **Identity Shift:** "I am an English speaker" (not "I study English")
5. **Repetition:** Daily practice builds neurological pathways

### TIGER BEHAVIORS IN CLASS

- Sits in front, engaged
- Volunteers answers frequently
- Makes mistakes confidently
- Laughs at own errors, tries again
- Helps peers without judgment
- Completes programs (high retention)

---

## 4.3 THE TRANSFORMATION PROCESS

### PHASE 1: AWARENESS (WEEK 1-2)

**Teacher Actions:**
- Identify Mouse behaviors explicitly
- Name it: "That's your Mouse Voice. Let's find your Tiger."
- Show contrast: Record student speaking quietly, then loudly
- No judgment, just observation

**Student Realization:**
- "Oh, I do apologize too much"
- "I didn't realize I was whispering"
- Awareness creates possibility for change

---

### PHASE 2: PRACTICE (WEEK 3-6)

**Teacher Actions:**
- Call out Mouse behaviors gently: "Try that again, louder."
- Praise Tiger behaviors loudly: "YES! That's Tiger Voice!"
- Create low-stakes practice (pair work before full class)
- Assign "Tiger Challenges" (speak to 3 strangers this week)

**Student Progress:**
- Gradual volume increase
- Fewer apologies
- More risk-taking in speech
- Small wins build confidence

---

### PHASE 3: INTEGRATION (WEEK 7-12)

**Teacher Actions:**
- Raise stakes (present to class, debates, mock interviews)
- Less correction, more encouragement
- Celebrate milestones ("Remember when you couldn't speak for 30 seconds?")
- Introduce to community (buddy system, networking events)

**Student Breakthrough:**
- Tiger becomes default mode
- Speaks without thinking about "Mouse"
- Confidence spills into other life areas (work, relationships)
- Identity shift: "I'm an English speaker now"

---

### PHASE 4: MASTERY (MONTH 4+)

**Teacher Actions:**
- Student becomes model for others
- Invite to help newer students
- Document success story
- Transition to advanced content

**Student State:**
- Natural, effortless Tiger Voice
- Speaks English without fear or translation
- Helps others transform
- Completes NET program, becomes alumni advocate

---

## 4.4 TEACHER INTERVENTIONS

### SCRIPT: "YOU'RE USING MOUSE VOICE"

**Situation:** Student apologizes for third time in 5 minutes

**Teacher:**
> "Hold on. I'm hearing your Mouse Voice right now. You just apologized three times. Tigers don't apologize for learning. You're trying, you're speaking, that's perfect. Let's try again, no 'sorry' this time. Ready?"

**Effect:** Student becomes aware, corrects behavior immediately

---

### SCRIPT: "SPEAK LOUDER"

**Situation:** Student mumbles answer

**Teacher:**
> "I need Tiger Voice. Say that again, louder. Pretend you're talking to someone across the room."

**Effect:** Forces projection, builds confidence through physicality

---

### SCRIPT: "NO TRANSLATION"

**Situation:** Student pauses to translate from Vietnamese

**Teacher:**
> "Stop! You're translating right now, I can tell. Just say it in English, even if it's broken. We'll fix it after. Go!"

**Effect:** Breaks translation habit, builds direct English thinking

---

### SCRIPT: "MISTAKES ARE WINS"

**Situation:** Student makes error and looks embarrassed

**Teacher:**
> "Yes! You made a mistake! That means you tried! Mistakes are data. Now we know what to fix. That's how learning works. Try it again."

**Effect:** Reframes errors as positive, reduces shame

# 5. CLASSROOM MANAGEMENT: THE ART

## 5.1 ENERGY MANAGEMENT

### THE PRINCIPLE

Every class has energy dynamics. Teacher's job is to **read the room** and adjust pace, activities, and tone accordingly.

### HIGH ENERGY CLASS

**Signs:**
- Students talking over each other
- Laughter, excitement
- Can't sit still

**Teacher Response:**
- Channel energy into activities (debates, games)
- Don't fight it — ride the wave
- Use competition (teams, points, prizes)

---

### LOW ENERGY CLASS

**Signs:**
- Silence, sluggish responses
- Checking phones
- Yawning

**Teacher Response:**
- **Physical movement:** Stand up, stretch, walk around
- **Change activity:** Stop lecture, start game
- **Increase stakes:** "Winner gets to leave 5 minutes early"
- **Personal connection:** Ask about their week, mood

---

### MIXED ENERGY CLASS

**Signs:**
- Some students engaged, others zoned out

**Teacher Response:**
- Call on quiet students specifically
- Pair high-energy with low-energy students
- Increase interactivity (more pair work, less lecture)

---

## 5.2 GROUP DYNAMICS

### BALANCING PERSONALITIES

**THE DOMINATOR:**
- Talks too much, interrupts others
- Solution: Set speaking time limits, "Let's hear from Nhan now"

**THE SILENT ONE:**
- Never volunteers
- Solution: Call on them directly with easy questions, build confidence

**THE CLOWN:**
- Jokes constantly, distracts class
- Solution: Channel humor into learning (make them perform role-plays)

**THE SKEPTIC:**
- "This won't work for me"
- Solution: Give them data, show success stories, address objections

---

### PAIR WORK STRATEGIES

**STRONG + WEAK PAIRING:**
- Pros: Weak student learns from strong
- Cons: Strong student may dominate
- When to use: When teaching new concept

**SIMILAR LEVEL PAIRING:**
- Pros: Equal participation
- Cons: No peer teaching benefit
- When to use: Free practice, debates

**RANDOM PAIRING:**
- Pros: Builds community, prevents cliques
- Cons: May create awkward pairs
- When to use: Warm-up activities

---

## 5.3 TIME MANAGEMENT

### THE 90-MINUTE CHALLENGE

Classes feel long. Attention spans are short. Solution: **Segment ruthlessly.**

**10-minute rule:** No single activity should last more than 10 minutes without a transition.

**Typical Mistakes:**
- ❌ 30-minute lecture (students zone out)
- ❌ 60-minute free conversation (loses structure)
- ❌ Skipping games to "save time" (kills engagement)

**Optimal Flow:**
1. Warm-up game (10 min)
2. Input (5 min)
3. Guided practice (5 min)
4. Free practice round 1 (15 min)
5. Mid-lesson game (10 min)
6. Free practice round 2 (20 min)
7. Break (5 min)
8. Free practice round 3 (15 min)
9. Final game (10 min)
10. Feedback (5 min)

**Total: 100 minutes** (allows buffer for overtime)

---

## 5.4 HANDLING DISRUPTIONS

### LATE ARRIVALS

**Protocol:**
- Don't stop class to acknowledge
- Quick nod or wave
- Continue teaching
- Catch them up during pair work

---

### PHONE USE

**Protocol:**
- Set rule: Phones away during class
- Exception: Using phone for vocabulary lookup
- If chronic: Address privately after class

---

### SIDE CONVERSATIONS (VIETNAMESE)

**Protocol:**
- Pause, make eye contact
- "Everything okay? Need help with something?"
- Redirect to English: "Let's discuss in English so everyone can learn"

---

### ARGUMENTS/CONFLICTS

**Protocol:**
- Stop immediately
- Separate if needed
- Address after class, not in front of others
- Find root cause (personality clash? Misunderstanding?)

---

## 5.5 INCLUSIVE TEACHING

### VISUAL LEARNERS

**Strategies:**
- Use board heavily (write key words)
- Show pictures, videos, diagrams
- Color-code grammar patterns

---

### AUDITORY LEARNERS

**Strategies:**
- Play audio clips frequently
- Repeat key phrases multiple times
- Use choral repetition

---

### KINESTHETIC LEARNERS

**Strategies:**
- Physical games (charades, board races)
- Role-plays with movement
- Gestures to reinforce vocabulary

---

### MIXED ABILITY CLASSES

**Challenge:** Some students A2, others B2

**Solution:**
- Tiered activities (different difficulty levels)
- Strong students help weak students (peer teaching)
- Individual check-ins during pair work

## 6.4 CRISIS #3: STUDENT SAYS "I DON'T KNOW"

**Situation:** Student gives up immediately without trying

> [Privately] "What's going on? Are you okay?"

**Situation:** Student interrupts, mocks, or challenges teacher

# 7. ASSESSMENT SYSTEM: MEASURING PROGRESS

## 7.1 OVERVIEW

NET uses **multiple assessment types** to track student progress holistically.

### ASSESSMENT TYPES

1. **Intake Assessment** (Before enrollment)
2. **Level Placement** (Diagnostic)
3. **Progress Checks** (Monthly)
4. **Final Assessment** (End of program)
5. **Self-Assessment** (Weekly reflection)

---

## 7.2 INTAKE ASSESSMENT (PRE-ENROLLMENT)

### PURPOSE

Determine:
- Current English level (A1-C2)
- Student archetype (Mouse vs Tiger)
- Specific pain points
- Learning goals
- Motivation level

### FORMAT (30-45 minutes)

**PART 1: Grammar Test (10 min)**
- 20 multiple-choice questions
- Tests A1-C1 grammar structures
- Auto-scored

**PART 2: Listening Test (10 min)**
- 3 audio clips (different difficulty levels)
- Comprehension questions
- Tests listening at 60% rule

**PART 3: Speaking Test (15 min)**
- 5 prompts, student speaks 1-2 minutes each
- Recorded for later evaluation
- Prompts:
  1. "Tell me about yourself"
  2. "Describe your typical workday"
  3. "What's your favorite hobby and why?"
  4. "Tell me about a time you had to speak English and it was difficult"
  5. "Why do you want to improve your English?"

**PART 4: Writing Test (10 min)**
- Write 150-word email responding to scenario
- Tests grammar, vocabulary, structure

### SCORING

**Grammar:** 0-100 (auto-scored)
**Listening:** 0-100 (auto-scored)
**Speaking:** 
- Fluency (1-10)
- Pronunciation (1-10)
- Grammar (1-10)
- Vocabulary (1-10)
- Confidence (1-10)

**Writing:**
- Grammar accuracy (1-10)
- Vocabulary range (1-10)
- Structure/organization (1-10)

### RESULTS

**Level Placement:**
- A1: Beginner (0-20)
- A2: Elementary (21-40)
- B1: Intermediate (41-60)
- B2: Upper Intermediate (61-80)
- C1: Advanced (81-100)

**Archetype:**
- Mouse Voice: Confidence score < 5
- Tiger Voice: Confidence score > 7

**Recommended Program:**
- Group vs. 1-on-1
- Intensity (2x/week vs. daily)
- Focus area (speaking, pronunciation, grammar)

---

## 7.3 PROGRESS CHECKS (MONTHLY)

### PURPOSE

Track improvement, adjust teaching strategy, maintain motivation

### FORMAT (15 minutes)

**PART 1: 2-Minute Speech (Recorded)**
- Same prompt every month: "What did you learn this month?"
- Compare to previous recordings
- Measure fluency, grammar, vocabulary improvement

**PART 2: Vocabulary Quiz (5 min)**
- 10 words from past month's lessons
- Use in sentences, not translate

**PART 3: Self-Assessment (5 min)**
- "How confident do you feel speaking English now?" (1-10)
- "What's your biggest win this month?"
- "What's still challenging?"

**PART 4: Teacher Observation (5 min)**
- Teacher rates:
  - Volume (Mouse→Tiger progress)
  - Participation frequency
  - Mistake-making comfort
  - Grammar accuracy improvement

### RESULTS SHARED WITH STUDENT

**Progress Report includes:**
- Audio comparison (Month 1 vs Month 2)
- Vocabulary retention rate
- Confidence score trend
- Teacher comments
- Action plan for next month

---

## 7.4 FINAL ASSESSMENT (END OF PROGRAM)

### PURPOSE

Demonstrate transformation, certify completion, celebrate success

# 8. THE BUDDY SYSTEM: PEER LEARNING

## 8.1 OVERVIEW

**The Buddy System pairs students for weekly 1-hour practice sessions outside of class.**

### WHY IT WORKS

- **Reduces pressure:** Speaking to peer vs. teacher/native speaker
- **Builds community:** Friendships form through shared struggle
- **Increases practice:** +1 hour/week of conversation
- **Improves retention:** Students with buddies complete at 90% rate (vs. 60% solo)

---

## 8.2 HOW IT WORKS

### PAIRING STRATEGY

**SIMILAR LEVEL:**
- Beginner + Beginner
- Intermediate + Intermediate
- Prevents one student from dominating

**OPPOSITE ARCHETYPES (IDEAL):**
- Mouse + Tiger (Tiger pulls Mouse out of shell)

**COMPATIBILITY FACTORS:**
- Schedule availability
- Interests (gaming, tech, business)
- Personality (introvert + extrovert balance)

### FIRST BUDDY SESSION (GUIDED)

**Teacher provides:**
- List of 10 conversation topics
- Timer guidelines (switch every 5 minutes)
- Feedback form to complete afterward

**Topics:**
1. Weekend plans
2. Favorite movie/show
3. Work/school stress
4. Learning English struggles
5. Vietnamese vs. English culture
6. Dream vacation destination
7. Tech or gaming interests
8. Food preferences
9. Personal goals for English
10. Funniest mistake made in English

**STRUCTURE:**
- 5 min: Student A talks, Student B listens
- 5 min: Student B talks, Student A listens
- 2 min: Switch topics
- Repeat 6 times (60 minutes total)

### ONGOING SESSIONS

**After first 3 weeks, students self-direct:**
- Choose own topics
- Adjust format (debates, storytelling, role-plays)
- Meet at café, park, online (Zoom)

**ACCOUNTABILITY:**
- Report to teacher: "Did you meet your buddy this week?"
- NET PET tracks buddy session attendance

# 9. PRONUNCIATION: THE 42 JOLLY PHONICS SOUNDS

## 9.1 OVERVIEW

**NET uses the Jolly Phonics method** to teach pronunciation systematically.

### WHY JOLLY PHONICS?

- Designed for English language learners
- Breaks English into 42 distinct sounds
- Each sound has gesture + story
- Proven effective for children (works for adults too)

### THE PROBLEM

Vietnamese has **~30 phonemes**. English has **44 phonemes**. Vietnamese speakers struggle with sounds that don't exist in Vietnamese:
- /θ/ (think)
- /ð/ (this)
- /v/ (very) — often pronounced as /w/
- /r/ (red) — often confused with /l/
- Ending consonants (Vietnamese rarely ends words with consonants)

---

## 9.2 THE 42 SOUNDS (GROUPED BY DIFFICULTY)

### GROUP 1: EASY (EXIST IN VIETNAMESE)

1. **s** (snake)
2. **a** (ant)
3. **t** (tap)
4. **p** (pop)
5. **i** (insect)
6. **n** (net)

### GROUP 2: MEDIUM (SIMILAR TO VIETNAMESE)

7. **c/k** (cat, kite)
8. **e** (egg)
9. **h** (hat)
10. **r** (rabbit) — tricky for Vietnamese!
11. **m** (mouse)
12. **d** (dog)

### GROUP 3: HARD (DON'T EXIST IN VIETNAMESE)

13. **g** (goat)
14. **o** (octopus)
15. **u** (umbrella)
16. **l** (lion) — often confused with /r/
17. **f** (fish)
18. **b** (ball)

### GROUP 4: VERY HARD (CRITICAL FOCUS)

19. **j** (jump)
20. **v** (van) — Vietnamese speakers say /w/
21. **w** (water)
22. **x** (box)
23. **y** (yes)
24. **z** (zebra)
25. **qu** (queen)

### GROUP 5: CONSONANT DIGRAPHS

26. **ch** (chip)
27. **sh** (ship)
28. **th** (think) — /θ/ sound (HARD!)
29. **th** (this) — /ð/ sound (HARD!)
30. **ng** (sing) — ending sound (HARD!)

### GROUP 6: VOWEL DIGRAPHS

31. **ai** (rain)
32. **ee** (see)
33. **igh** (night)
34. **oa** (boat)
35. **oo** (book/moon) — two sounds!
36. **ar** (car)
37. **or** (fork)
38. **ur** (burn)
39. **ow** (cow)
40. **oi** (coin)
41. **ear** (hear)
42. **air** (hair)

---

## 9.3 PRONUNCIATION DRILLS (WEEKLY FOCUS)

**Each week, NET focuses on 3-5 sounds from the 42.**

**WEEK 1: TH SOUNDS (/θ/ and /ð/)**

**Lesson:**
1. Demonstrate tongue placement (between teeth)
2. Practice minimal pairs:
   - "Think" vs. "Sink"
   - "This" vs. "Dis"
   - "Mouth" vs. "Mouse"
3. Tongue twister:
   > "Three thin thieves thought they saw the throne."
4. Record student saying it, compare to native speaker

**WEEK 2: R vs. L**

**Lesson:**
1. Show lip position for /r/ (rounded)
2. Show tongue position for /l/ (touches roof)
3. Practice minimal pairs:
   - "Right" vs. "Light"
   - "Red" vs. "Led"
   - "Collect" vs. "Correct"
4. Sentence drill:
   > "The red light is on the right."

**WEEK 3: V vs. W**

**Lesson:**
1. /v/ = teeth touch bottom lip
2. /w/ = lips rounded, no teeth
3. Practice:
   - "Very" vs. "Worry" (different sounds!)
   - "Vest" vs. "West"
4. Sentence:
   > "We were very worried about the weather."

---

## 9.4 PRONUNCIATION FEEDBACK PROTOCOL

**IN CLASS:**
- Don't interrupt mid-speech for pronunciation
- Note errors, address in feedback segment
- Demonstrate correct sound, have student repeat

**BUDDY SESSIONS:**
- Students record each other
- Compare to native speaker audio (from NET app)
- Practice together, give peer feedback

**NET PET INTEGRATION:**
- Students record daily pronunciation drills
- AI analyzes pronunciation accuracy
- Provides score + specific feedback
