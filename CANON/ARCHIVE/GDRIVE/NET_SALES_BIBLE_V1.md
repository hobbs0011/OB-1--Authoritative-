## 1. SALES PHILOSOPHY

### 1.1 WE DON'T "SELL" - WE DIAGNOSE & PRESCRIBE

**The NET Sales Approach:**

NET is not a transactional business. We're doctors, not salespeople.

**Traditional Sales:**
1. Pitch product
2. Overcome objections
3. Close deal
4. Move to next customer

**NET Sales:**
1. **Diagnose** the student's exact situation
2. **Understand** their psychology and barriers
3. **Prescribe** the right solution for THEM
4. **Support** them through the decision
5. **Partner** with them long-term

###  1.2 CORE SALES PRINCIPLES

**PRINCIPLE 1: NO PRESSURE, ONLY CLARITY**
- Never use high-pressure tactics
- Never use artificial urgency ("only 2 spots left!")
- Give students time to decide
- Our confidence in the method IS the sales pitch

**PRINCIPLE 2: QUALIFICATION OVER VOLUME**
- We don't want "anyone with money"
- We want committed, coachable students
- Better to turn someone away than take wrong fit
- Quality > Quantity

**PRINCIPLE 3: EDUCATION-FIRST SELLING**
- Teach them about Natural Acquisition
- Explain WHY traditional methods failed them
- Show them the science (Krashen, comprehensible input)
- Informed students convert better and stay longer

**PRINCIPLE 4: AUTHENTIC EMPATHY**
- We've been there (Michael's story)
- We understand Vietnamese psychology deeply
- Fear, shame, conditioning are REAL
- Acknowledge pain before offering solution

**PRINCIPLE 5: RESULTS-BASED PRICING**
- Pricing reflects value delivered
- Investment positioning (not "cost")
- Payment plans for accessibility
- ROI framing (career advancement > course fee)

## 2. THE ASSESSMENT PROCESS

### 2.1 ASSESSMENT OVERVIEW

**Duration:** 30-45 minutes  
**Format:** In-person (preferred) or Video call  
**Goal:** Comprehensive diagnosis + relationship building  
**Outcome:** Personalized recommendation + enrollment decision

### 2.2 ASSESSMENT STRUCTURE (6 PARTS)

**PART 1: WELCOME & RAPPORT (5 minutes)**

**Script:**
> "Hi [Name], thanks so much for coming today. I'm Michael. Before we jump into the assessment, I want you to know—this is a judgment-free space. There are no wrong answers here. My job is to understand where you are now and help you get where you want to go. Sound good?"

**Goals:**
- Ease anxiety
- Build trust
- Set relaxed tone
- Establish safety

**Questions:**
- "How did you hear about NET?"
- "What made you interested enough to book this?"
- "Have you tried learning English before? How did that go?"

**PART 2: MOTIVATION DEEP DIVE (8-10 minutes)**

**Script:**
> "Let's talk about WHY you want to improve your English. Not the surface reason—the REAL reason. If I could wave a magic wand and you spoke perfect English tomorrow, what changes in your life?"

**Critical Questions:**
1. **Career Impact:** "How is English affecting your work right now?"
2. **Emotional Impact:** "How do you FEEL when you need to speak English?"
3. **Time Horizon:** "When do you need to see results? Is there a deadline?"
4. **Past Failures:** "You mentioned you tried before. What went wrong?"
5. **Commitment Level:** "If I can show you a path that works, are you ready to commit?"

**What We're Listening For:**
- ✅ Specific goals (promotion, client meeting, travel)
- ✅ Emotional urgency (embarrassment, frustration, fear)
- ✅ Timeline with real stakes
- ✅ Recognition that past methods didn't work
- ✅ Readiness to invest time and money

**Red Flags (May Not Be Good Fit):**
- ❌ Vague goals ("just want to improve")
- ❌ No urgency ("someday would be nice")
- ❌ Blame mentality ("teachers were bad")
- ❌ Magic pill thinking ("quick and easy method")

**PART 3: LISTENING ASSESSMENT (5-7 minutes)**

**Script:**
> "Okay, now let's check your listening comprehension. I'm going to read you a short story—just a normal conversation, nothing technical. Don't worry about understanding every word. Just focus on the main ideas."

**Method:**
- Read "Sarah's Morning" passage (from assessment deck)
- Natural speed, clear pronunciation
- Ask 3 comprehension questions
- Note: vocabulary, context inference, confidence

**Scoring:**
- 3/3 correct: Strong listening (B2+)
- 2/3 correct: Intermediate (B1)
- 1/3 correct: Lower intermediate (A2-B1)
- 0/3 correct: Beginner (A1-A2)

**PART 4: SPEAKING ASSESSMENT (8-10 minutes)**

**Script:**
> "Great! Now I want to hear YOU speak. I'm going to record this—not to judge you, but so I can give you specific feedback later. Ready? Just relax and tell me about yourself. Your name, your job, why you want to learn English. Speak for 1-2 minutes."

**Recording Note:**
This recording is CRITICAL. Use phone to record audio. Save with student name + date.

**What We're Assessing:**
- **Volume:** Can we hear them clearly?
- **Fluency:** Pauses, hesitations, flow
- **Pronunciation:** Specific Vietnamese errors (th, v/w, final consonants)
- **Confidence:** Body language, eye contact, apologizing
- **Grammar:** (Note, but don't emphasize)

**Follow-Up Question:**
> "Describe this picture to me." [Show Saigon morning scene]

**Scoring Notes:**
- Volume 1-10 (Vietnamese speakers often 3-5)
- Fluency 1-10 (hesitations, translation pauses)
- Confidence 1-10 (body language, voice quality)
- Pronunciation: Note 3-5 specific issues

**PART 5: SOFT SKILLS ASSESSMENT (5 minutes)**

**Script:**
> "Last few questions—these are about your learning style and mindset."

**Questions:**
1. "When you speak English and don't know a word, what do you do?"
   - (Looking for: adaptability vs. freeze response)

2. "Do you practice English outside of class? How often?"
   - (Looking for: self-discipline, current habits)

3. "On a scale of 1-10, how committed are you to practicing 1 hour daily?"
   - (Looking for: realistic self-assessment)

4. "How do you feel about making mistakes in front of others?"
   - (Looking for: fear level, openness to feedback)

**PART 6: FEEDBACK & RECOMMENDATION (8-10 minutes)**

**Script:**
> "Okay [Name], here's what I observed. First, the good news: [specific strengths]. Now, areas we'll work on: [specific challenges]. Based on what I've seen and what you've told me about your goals, here's what I recommend..."

**Recommendation Framework:**

**IF Strong Motivation + Intermediate Level + High Commitment:**
→ **Recommend:** 1-on-1 Program
- Faster results
- Personalized curriculum
- Intensive focus on their specific goals

**IF Moderate Motivation + Beginner-Intermediate + Social Learner:**
→ **Recommend:** Group Program
- Community support
- More affordable
- Buddy system built-in

**IF Low Confidence + Fear + Low Volume (Like Nhan):**
→ **Recommend:** 1-on-1 Program (Essential)
- Safe space to build confidence
- Volume training intensive
- Graduated exposure

**IMPORTANT:** Always explain WHY you're recommending what you're recommending. Students need to understand the logic.

## 4. OBJECTION HANDLING

**What They're Really Saying:**
- "I don't see the value yet"
- "I'm not sure it will work"
- "I have other financial priorities"

**Response:**

> You mentioned that [specific consequence—missed promotion, embarrassment in meetings, can't communicate with clients]. If you could fix that problem in 3-6 months, what would that be worth?

> [Pause]

> Here's how I think about it: This is an investment in your CAREER. If confident English helps you get promoted, land a better job, or communicate with international clients—the ROI is massive.

It's half the cost of 1-on-1, but you still get NET's full method, AI coaching, and community support. You can always upgrade to 1-on-1 later if you want faster results."

### 4.2 OBJECTION HANDLING PRINCIPLES

**PRINCIPLE 1: LISTEN FIRST**
- Don't interrupt
- Don't get defensive
- Acknowledge their concern as valid

**PRINCIPLE 2: IDENTIFY THE REAL CONCERN**
- Often the stated objection isn't the real one
- Ask clarifying questions
- Get to the emotional truth

**PRINCIPLE 3: EMPATHIZE**
- "I understand..."
- "That makes sense..."
- "I'd feel the same way..."

**PRINCIPLE 4: REFRAME**
- Show the situation from a different angle
- Connect to their stated goals
- Use their own words back to them

**PRINCIPLE 5: CALL TO ACTION**
- Don't leave it hanging
- Ask for the decision
- Make next steps clear

## 6. CLOSING TECHNIQUES

### 6.1 THE ASSUMPTIVE CLOSE

**When to Use:** Student is clearly interested, just needs gentle push

**Script:**
> "Great, so it sounds like the [1-on-1/group] program is the right fit. Let's get you scheduled. Are Tuesday evenings or Saturday mornings better for you?"

**Key:** Assume the sale, move to logistics. If they object, they'll tell you.

### 6.2 THE SUMMARY CLOSE

**When to Use:** Student seems overwhelmed with information

**Script:**
> "Let me summarize what we've covered: You want to [specific goal], you're currently at [level], and the biggest challenge is [specific issue]. The [recommended program] will address all of that through [key features]. The investment is [price] with [payment option]. Everything make sense? Ready to get started?"

**Key:** Recap everything clearly, then ask directly.

### 6.3 THE ALTERNATIVE CHOICE CLOSE

**When to Use:** Student is deciding between options

**Script:**
> "So which program feels right to you—the 1-on-1 for faster results and personalization, or the group for community support and affordability?"

**Key:** Both options assume they're enrolling. Choice paralysis vs. yes/no.

### 6.4 THE PUPPY DOG CLOSE

**When to Use:** Student is nervous about committing

**Script:**
> "I totally understand the hesitation. Here's what I suggest: Let's do the first month. If after 4 sessions you're not seeing improvement or you feel like this isn't for you, I'll refund you in full. But I'm betting that after 4 sessions, you're going to be amazed at your progress. Sound fair?"

**Key:** Remove risk, make it easy to say yes.

### 6.5 THE URGENCY CLOSE (Honest Version)

**When to Use:** Student is dragging feet with no real reason

**Script:**
> "I want to be direct with you. Every day you wait is another day you're stuck where you are—nervous in meetings, avoiding English, missing opportunities. I can help you change that, but only if you take action. We have openings this week. After that, it's a 2-week wait. What do you want to do?"

**Key:** Real urgency (calendar fills up), not fake scarcity.

### 6.6 WHAT NOT TO DO

**NEVER:**
- ❌ Beg or plead
- ❌ Discount unprompted
- ❌ Badmouth competitors
- ❌ Lie or exaggerate
- ❌ Push if they're clearly not ready
- ❌ Make them feel guilty

**REMEMBER:**
- Some people aren't ready. That's okay.
- No means "not now," not "never."
- Maintain relationship even if they don't enroll.
- Quality students > quantity of students.

## 10. SALES TRAINING

### 10.1 SALES SKILLS TO DEVELOP

**SKILL 1: ACTIVE LISTENING**
- Listen more than talk (70/30 rule)
- Ask clarifying questions
- Paraphrase to confirm understanding
- Notice emotional cues

**SKILL 2: EMPATHY & RAPPORT**
- Mirror body language
- Use their words and phrases
- Share personal stories
- Acknowledge emotions

**SKILL 3: DIAGNOSTIC THINKING**
- Identify root causes, not symptoms
- Ask "why" questions
- Connect dots between psychology and behavior
- Prescribe, don't pitch

**SKILL 4: OBJECTION HANDLING**
- Anticipate objections
- Reframe concerns
- Use stories/examples
- Stay calm and confident

**SKILL 5: CLOSING**
- Ask for the sale directly
- Don't fear rejection
- Know when to push, when to back off
- Follow up persistently
