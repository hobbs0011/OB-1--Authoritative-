**Rule:** Never declare the edge found. When a scout returns claiming completion, the only valid question is: *"What did you see from the new horizon?"*

## ROMAN PRECEDENT

The Romans understood this paradox intuitively. They never declared a final frontier. They built the *Limes* — not walls against the end of the world, but walls against the unknown. Every *limes* was an admission: *the world goes further than we can reach.*

- **Hadrian's Wall**: Britannia didn't end at Caledonia. The wall marked where Rome's reach exceeded its grasp.
- **The Rhine**: Germania swallowed three legions at Teutoburg in 9 AD. Augustus reportedly roamed the halls at night crying *"Quinctilius Varus, give me back my legions!"* The empire never crossed the Rhine again. The Germans remained unknown.
- **Parthia**: Rome traded with the Persians as equals, not subjects. The Silk Road extended further east than any legion ever marched. China existed for centuries without Roman record.

The *Exploratores* rode ahead of the column knowing they would never find the end. Their job was not to conquer the world. Their job was to ensure the legion never marched blind.
