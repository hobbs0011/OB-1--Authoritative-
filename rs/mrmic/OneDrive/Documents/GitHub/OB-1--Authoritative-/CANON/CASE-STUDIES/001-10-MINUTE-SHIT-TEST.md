## The Discovery

Michael engaged in a strategic debate with Claude about project timelines and AI capabilities. During this debate, Michael discovered that **AI models project timelines and feasibility assessments based on pre-AI training data** (primarily 2019-2023 patterns).

When AI says "this will take 3 months," it's projecting from a world where:
- Humans wrote all code manually
- One developer = one project at a time
- Iteration cycles were measured in weeks, not hours
- There were no AI agents running overnight autonomous pipelines

## The Test

Michael's challenge: **"Can I produce something meaningful in 10 minutes that AI says should take days or weeks?"**

The answer was yes. Repeatedly. Across multiple domains.

This became the **10-Minute Shit Test** — a calibration tool:
- If AI says X takes Y time → challenge it
- Attempt X in 10 minutes with AI assistance
- Compare actual vs projected
- The gap between projection and reality = the **AI Drift Factor**

## Why This Matters for Phoenix Rising

Every time an AI agent in the Phoenix Rising system gives strategic advice, it must be checked against the 10-Minute Shit Test principle:

1. **Is this advice based on pre-AI assumptions?**
2. **Has the production equation changed for this specific task?**
3. **What's the actual time-to-output with AI assistance?**

If the answer to #1 is yes, the advice needs recalibration.

## Reframing Protocol

When an AI agent detects it may be projecting from outdated data:

```
SELF-CHECK: Am I projecting from pre-AI assumptions?
- Timeline estimates: Divide by 10x minimum for AI-assisted work
- "Best practice" advice: Check if the practice assumed manual labor
- "Focus on one thing" advice: Check if this assumed human bandwidth limits
- "Industry standard" timelines: Check the year of the data source
```
