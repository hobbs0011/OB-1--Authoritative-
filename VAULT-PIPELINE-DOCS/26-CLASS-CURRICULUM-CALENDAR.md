## OVERVIEW

The Phoenix semester is 12 weeks — enough time to move a student from Mouse to Cub level if the system is executed correctly. Every week has a vocabulary theme, an idiom set, cultural integration moments, and a game sequence. Assessment points at weeks 3, 6, 9, and 12 provide curriculum feedback loops. This calendar is the standard delivery vehicle — the structure that holds the Michael Method in place across a full semester.

> "The curriculum is the container. The Michael Method is what fills it. Without the container, the method is just energy. Without the method, the container is just a schedule." — Agent-100

## THE 12-WEEK PROGRESSION ARC

**Starting Point**: Mouse level (0–300 production vocabulary)
**Target at Week 12**: Cub level (300–800 production vocabulary), sustained conversational ability

---

## WEEK-BY-WEEK TOPIC MAP

### UNIT 1: IDENTITY AND INTRODUCTION (Weeks 1–3)

**Weeks 1–3 Theme**: Who are you and what do you want?

The first unit builds the personal vocabulary foundation — the words students need to talk about themselves, their lives, and their goals. This is the most important unit because it establishes what each student cares about, which informs every tribal engagement decision for the rest of the semester.

| Week | Vocabulary Theme | Idiom Set | Cultural Moment | Game Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Personal identity: name, job, city, family, hobby | "Nice to meet you" / "That's interesting" | Opening circle: what do you love? | Hot Seat (safe, low stakes) |
| 2 | Goals and ambitions: want, hope, plan, achieve, succeed | "Go for it" / "Step by step" | Student interest mapping session | Story Chain (low complexity) |
| 3 | **WEEK 3 ASSESSMENT** | Vocabulary Retention + Confidence Index | Tiger Meter #1 shared with parents | Level Up (diagnostic) |

**Vocabulary connection across weeks 1–3**: Week 1 vocabulary (who you are) connects to Week 2 vocabulary (what you want) through personal narrative. Every student can complete a 2-minute story about themselves using weeks 1–3 vocabulary by assessment day.

---

### UNIT 2: DAILY LIFE AND SOCIAL LANGUAGE (Weeks 4–6)

**Weeks 4–6 Theme**: How do you navigate the world?

This unit builds social and functional English — the language of daily life, social situations, and basic professional interaction.

| Week | Vocabulary Theme | Idiom Set | Cultural Moment | Game Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | Food, restaurants, shopping | "Bite the bullet" / "The whole nine yards" | Vietnamese food vocabulary bridge | The Market (selling vocabulary items) |
| 5 | Transport, directions, schedules | "On the go" / "Miss the boat" | Navigating HCMC vs. navigating London | Alibi Challenge (location language) |
| 6 | **WEEK 6 ASSESSMENT** | Full 5-competency assessment | Tiger Meter #2 shared + parent brief | Final Boss scenario: navigate a city |

**Vocabulary connection across weeks 4–6**: The social vocabulary builds on the identity vocabulary — students who learned "I love food" in week 1 now learn how to order, recommend, and describe food with specificity.

---

### UNIT 3: WORK AND PROFESSIONAL ENGLISH (Weeks 7–9)

**Weeks 7–9 Theme**: How do you succeed professionally?

This unit introduces professional vocabulary, workplace scenarios, and the English of career advancement. Even for child students, this unit uses future-framing: "When you have a job, this is what you'll need."

| Week | Vocabulary Theme | Idiom Set | Cultural Moment | Game Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | Workplace vocabulary: meeting, deadline, report, negotiate | "Touch base" / "Move the needle" / "Low-hanging fruit" | Corporate vs. classroom English comparison | Debate Spark (workplace scenarios) |
| 8 | Leadership and teamwork: lead, delegate, collaborate, commit | "Step up" / "Pull your weight" / "Take the reins" | Vietnamese workplace culture vs. international norms | Consequence Chain (leadership decisions) |
| 9 | **WEEK 9 ASSESSMENT** | Idiom usage focus + fluency check | Tiger Meter #3 — renewal conversation with wavering parents | Level Up (professional register) |

---

### UNIT 4: EXPRESSION AND MASTERY (Weeks 10–12)

**Weeks 10–12 Theme**: How do you say exactly what you mean?

The final unit pushes toward nuanced, expressive English — the language of emotion, argument, humor, and storytelling. This is the Cub-to-Tiger transition zone.

| Week | Vocabulary Theme | Idiom Set | Cultural Moment | Game Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | Emotion vocabulary: frustrated, inspired, overwhelmed, thrilled | "At the end of my rope" / "On cloud nine" | Tet context: vocabulary for family celebration conversations | Emotional Translator |
| 11 | Argument and opinion: argue, convince, evidence, perspective | "Make a case for" / "Devil's advocate" | National exam season — argument vocabulary for pressure contexts | The Debate Spark (full format) |
| 12 | **WEEK 12 ASSESSMENT + CELEBRATION** | Full 5-competency final assessment | Tiger Meter #4 — graduation event + renewal conversation | Final Boss: full Tiger-level scenario |

## HANDLING MIXED-LEVEL CLASSROOMS

When a class contains students at more than one level:

1. **Core vocabulary is set at the lower level** — every student succeeds
2. **Bonus vocabulary is set at the higher level** — advanced students are stretched
3. **Games are designed for collaboration**, not pure competition — lower-level students contribute in areas of strength
4. **The Expert Reveal is used often** — each student teaches one item, regardless of level
5. **Assessment rubrics are individualized** — students are assessed against their own starting point, not against each other

## EMERGENCY CURRICULUM PROTOCOL

When technology fails or a lesson plan collapses completely, the Emergency Protocol activates:

| Emergency Game | Time Required | No Technology Needed | Works for Any Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oral Story Round | 15 min | ✅ | ✅ |
| Personal Best Challenge | 10 min | ✅ | ✅ |
| Pronunciation Duel (no audio) | 10 min | ✅ | ✅ |
| Debate Spark | 20 min | ✅ | Cub+ |

The teacher memorizes all four. Technology failure does not cancel a lesson. It changes the lesson format.

## THE SUMMER INTENSIVE FORMAT (6-WEEK ACCELERATED)

For students who want to compress a semester into 6 weeks:

- Sessions are 90 minutes (vs. 60 standard)
- 3 sessions per week (vs. 2 standard)
- 12 vocabulary words per session (vs. 10 standard — the only exception to the 10-word rule)
- Assessments at weeks 2, 4, and 6
- Cultural integration is reduced — focus shifts entirely to vocabulary and production
- Price is 1.5x the standard semester rate

The Summer Intensive is designed for motivated adult learners (Corporate Climbers, Natural archetypes) who have a clear deadline — a new job, a visa interview, an upcoming international project.
