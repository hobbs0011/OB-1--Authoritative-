## THE TEN FAILURE MODES

### 1. TECHNOLOGY FAILURE

**Description**: Projector dies. Internet drops. CHATTY PRIME loses connection mid-session. Audio cuts out during pronunciation drill.

**Root Cause**: Over-dependence on single-point technology without analog backup. Vietnam infrastructure variability is not a bug — it is a permanent condition.

**Real Example Scenario**: Midway through a BlueTech corporate session, the classroom WiFi dropped. CHATTY PRIME went offline. The vocabulary game froze on the screen. Fifteen professionals stared at a blank screen. The teacher had no offline backup plan.

**Immediate Fix**: Switch immediately to "The Oral Round" — teacher leads a verbal vocab review without any technology. Use the whiteboard. Own the room with presence. Never apologize for the tech failure — reframe as "now we go old school, which is actually harder."

**Prevention Protocol**: Every lesson plan includes a zero-tech fallback. Physical flashcards available. 3 oral games memorized that require no equipment. Technology is an enhancement, not the foundation.

---

### 2. STUDENT ENGAGEMENT COLLAPSE

**Description**: The class goes silent. Eyes glaze. Energy drops to zero. No one volunteers. Responses become monosyllabic.

**Root Cause**: Pacing was too slow, topic was irrelevant to the students' lives, or the teacher lost personal energy and the room mirrored it.

**Real Example Scenario**: A lesson on "office communication vocabulary" delivered to a group of 14-year-olds. By minute 15, two students had their heads down. The remaining four gave one-word answers. The teacher kept pushing the content harder. The class collapsed by minute 25.

**Immediate Fix**: Stop the planned content immediately. Deploy Pattern Interrupt: stand up, change physical position, ask one wildly off-topic question ("If you could fight any animal and win, which one?"). Rebuild energy before returning to language.

**Prevention Protocol**: Topic relevance check before every lesson. Pre-class student interest scan. Energy match protocol — teacher sets energy baseline two minutes before class starts.

---

### 3. CONTENT MISMATCH

**Description**: The vocabulary level is wrong — either too advanced (students shut down) or too basic (students disengage from boredom).

**Root Cause**: Incorrect intake assessment. Assuming all students in a level share the same actual competency. Failing to re-assess after progression milestones.

**Real Example Scenario**: A newly enrolled adult student was placed in a Cub-level group based on self-reported English ability. First class revealed she was operating at Mouse level. The group moved at twice her processing speed. She did not return after week two.

**Immediate Fix**: In-class level rescue — immediately pair with the strongest student for peer scaffolding. Assign a differentiated task. Never expose the mismatch to the group.

**Prevention Protocol**: Mandatory verbal intake assessment before group placement. Level recalibration at weeks 3 and 6. Private check-in with any student who goes quiet in first two classes.

---

### 4. TEACHER DRIFT

**Description**: The teacher abandons the lesson structure mid-session and follows a conversational tangent, losing the vocabulary objectives and game sequence.

**Root Cause**: Over-confidence in improvisation. The tangent feels productive — the students are engaged — but the lesson objectives are never reached.

**Real Example Scenario**: A lesson on "negotiation vocabulary" drifted into a 20-minute discussion about whether AI would replace jobs. Students were engaged. No vocabulary from the lesson plan was practiced. The teacher reported it as "a great class."

**Immediate Fix**: Teacher self-audit at minute 20: "What vocabulary have students actually produced?" If the answer is none or one, redirect immediately.

**Prevention Protocol**: 3-word anchor cards on the teacher's desk. If those 3 words have not been used by students by minute 20, the lesson is drifting. Weekly lesson review where planned vs. actual content is compared.

---

### 5. PACING ERROR

**Description**: Teacher moves too fast (students left behind, confusion builds silently) or too slow (students disengage, boredom sets in).

**Root Cause**: Not reading the room. Focusing on content delivery rather than student response calibration.

**Real Example Scenario**: A Tiger-level class was given 10 new vocabulary words in 8 minutes with one example each. By word 6, students were nodding but not processing. Teacher tested retention at end of class: average recall was 3 words.

**Immediate Fix**: The 3-Word Rule — confirm 3 words are fully owned before moving to the next 3. Owned = student can use it in a sentence they created, not recalled.

**Prevention Protocol**: 10-5-5-3 physics applied strictly. Pace is set by the slowest successful student, not the fastest.

---

### 6. VOCABULARY OVERLOAD

**Description**: More than 10 new words introduced in a single session. Students cannot process, retain, or use any of them effectively.

**Root Cause**: Teacher anxiety about "covering enough content." Confusing input volume with learning outcome.

**Real Example Scenario**: A well-prepared teacher presented 18 vocabulary words across a 90-minute corporate class. End-of-session check: students could recall 4 and use 1 correctly.

**Immediate Fix**: Immediately cut to the 5 highest-utility words from the list. Abandon the rest for next session.

**Prevention Protocol**: Hard cap of 10 vocabulary items per session, documented in lesson plan. Any teacher who submits a lesson plan with more than 10 vocabulary targets gets the plan returned for revision.

---

### 7. ENERGY MISMATCH

**Description**: Teacher comes in high-energy to a low-energy group (or vice versa). The room rejects the frequency.

**Root Cause**: No pre-class room-reading. Same energy template applied regardless of what students bring in.

**Real Example Scenario**: A Monday 7:30am corporate class received the same high-voltage opening as a Friday afternoon teen class. The professionals sat stiffly. Two checked their phones within the first five minutes.

**Immediate Fix**: Within the first 60 seconds, mirror the room's energy, then lift it by 15% — not 100%. Match before you lead.

**Prevention Protocol**: 90-second energy audit before class starts. Observe students as they arrive. Set energy level accordingly. Morning corporate = measured, professional warm-up. After-school teens = controlled chaos from minute one.

---

### 8. PARENT INTERVENTION DURING LESSON

**Description**: A parent enters the classroom during a session to ask questions, deliver food, check on their child, or otherwise interrupt the flow state.

**Root Cause**: No clear boundary protocol communicated at enrollment. Vietnamese parent culture treats the classroom as accessible space.

**Real Example Scenario**: During a peak engagement moment — students were mid-game, energy was high — a mother entered to hand her son a snack and ask the teacher about homework. The class energy collapsed. It took 8 minutes to rebuild.

**Immediate Fix**: Politely but immediately signal the parent to wait outside. Never engage in a full conversation mid-lesson. Assign the class a quick task ("Write one sentence using today's vocabulary") and step out for 90 seconds max.

**Prevention Protocol**: Parent boundary protocol communicated clearly at enrollment. Physical sign on classroom door during session. Designated parent update time (after class, 5 minutes maximum, scheduled).

---

### 9. AI HALLUCINATION EVENT

**Description**: CHATTY PRIME generates incorrect information — a wrong definition, a culturally inappropriate example, a pronunciation guide for a word that doesn't exist, or an incorrect student assessment.

**Root Cause**: Large language model limitations. CHATTY PRIME operates at the edge of its training data when asked highly specific, culturally nuanced, or real-time factual questions.

**Real Example Scenario**: During a lesson on idioms, CHATTY PRIME defined "bite the bullet" as "to eat metal when you are angry." Three students wrote it down before the teacher caught it.

**Immediate Fix**: Teacher immediately flags the output verbally ("Great effort, CHATTY — let me add to that"). Correct in real-time. Never embarrass the AI system in front of students — frame it as "the AI is still learning, just like us."

**Prevention Protocol**: Teacher reviews AI-generated content before displaying it to students. CHATTY PRIME outputs are treated as drafts, not final authority. Human verification layer is non-negotiable.

---

### 10. COMPLETE SESSION COLLAPSE

**Description**: Everything fails simultaneously. Technology down, student energy zero, teacher losing composure, content irrelevant, no backup plan. The lesson is unrecoverable.

**Root Cause**: Cascading failure. Usually triggered by one failure mode that the teacher failed to address, leading to a second, then a third.

**Real Example Scenario**: Technology failed during a class with a group of reluctant teenagers. Teacher tried to push planned content without tech. Students refused to engage. Teacher became visibly frustrated. Three students started talking to each other in Vietnamese. Teacher gave up and turned the last 20 minutes into free conversation time. Learning outcome: zero.

**Immediate Fix**: Declare a "Phoenix Lesson" openly. Say: "Today the normal class is cancelled. Today we're doing something different." Run the three emergency protocols: Oral Story Round (students build a story one sentence at a time), Personal Best Challenge (each student teaches the class one English word they know), and the Debate Spark (one wild question, students argue in English for 5 minutes).

**Prevention Protocol**: Every teacher memorizes the 3 emergency protocols. Session collapse is not a teaching failure if the recovery is executed correctly. The collapse itself becomes the lesson.

| Parent Intervention | Door opens | Immediate | 90-second exit |
